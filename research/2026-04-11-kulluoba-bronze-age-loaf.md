# Research Note: Kulluoba Bronze Age Loaf

## Question

How should the newly published Kulluoba bread from Early Bronze Age Anatolia be
turned into a practical historical recipe for this repo?

## Short answer

The best first version is a `small, thick, lightly risen oval loaf` built
around:

- wholemeal spelt as the practical stand-in for emmer,
- wholemeal wheat as supporting flour,
- a small amount of ground lentil,
- restrained hydration,
- and a very small dose of instant yeast plus a long, cool bulk to mimic the
  weak lift suggested by the archaeological bread.

This should be treated as a `modern homage` to the Kulluoba loaf, not a literal
reconstruction.

## What the paper tells us

The new PLOS ONE paper is unusually rich in recipe-relevant detail.

Main findings:

- the bread was `oval`, about `12 cm` across and `2.5 cm` thick,
- it was deliberately broken, carbonized, and buried under clean red earth near
  a doorway,
- the principal cereal component was `emmer wheat`,
- `lentil` was present in small amounts,
- a `rachis fragment` survived inside the bread, indicating unsieved or only
  partly processed grain,
- and irregular air voids suggest kneading plus possible fermentation or
  leavening.

The paper also argues that the preserved starch structure suggests the bread
may have been baked `briefly at high temperature`, and that despite the
abundance of barley in the site archaeobotanical material, `barley does not
appear to have been used in this bread`.

## What remains uncertain

Important uncertainties:

- the paper does not identify a specific leavening culture,
- it does not provide an exact flour ratio,
- it does not tell us the exact milling grade beyond showing coarse and
  unsieved character,
- and it does not let us reconstruct the original baking installation in a
  fully precise way.

So the house recipe needs to stay honest about where evidence ends and
interpretation begins.

## House adaptation choices

## Grain choice

The archaeometric evidence points strongly to `emmer wheat` as the main grain.
Because emmer is not yet in the pantry, the most practical stand-in currently
on hand is `wholemeal spelt flour`.

This is an inference from the current pantry, not a claim that spelt and emmer
are identical.

To keep the loaf workable:

- spelt should be the main stand-in,
- wholemeal bread wheat can support it,
- lentil should stay clearly minor.

## Lentil level

The paper only supports `small amounts` of lentil. So the lentil should read as
an accent, not a defining bulk ingredient.

House conclusion:

- start around `5% to 6%` lentil flour by total flour weight.

## Fermentation choice

The paper says the air pockets may indicate fermentation or leavening, but it
does not establish a specific sourdough ecology.

So the safest home-kitchen move is:

- use a `tiny amount` of instant yeast,
- keep bulk long and cool,
- and aim for `light aeration`, not a modern lofty loaf.

That is a practical inference, not a direct claim from the paper.

## Shape and bake

Because the original bread was small, oval, and thick rather than a tall loaf,
the house version should not be expanded to the repo's usual large boule scale.

The best match is:

- one compact oval loaf,
- around `12 to 14 cm` across,
- about `2.5 to 3 cm` thick before baking,
- baked hot on preheated cast iron.

## House conclusion

The strongest first recipe for this repo is a:

`Kulluoba-inspired Bronze Age ritual loaf`

with:

- wholemeal spelt as emmer stand-in,
- wholemeal wheat as support,
- a small dose of hand-ground lentil,
- weak yeast lift,
- and a hot cast-iron bake.
