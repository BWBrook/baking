# Research Note: Georgian-Style Wholemeal Household Loaf

## Question

How can a first plain wholemeal loaf for this repository be grounded in real
historical bread context while still being practical in a modern home kitchen?

## Short answer

A good first pass is a Georgian-style household loaf rather than a strict
reconstruction. The historical sources point to a bread culture that clearly
distinguished finer white loaves from wheaten and household breads, and they
also show sponge-based yeast methods, repeated proving, and a hot initial bake.
For this repo, a practical adaptation is a 100% wholemeal wheat loaf using
instant yeast, a sponge, gentle development, and Dutch-oven baking at the
house-standard 750 g flour scale. The hydration and exact timings are modern
inferences based on wholemeal handling, not a direct historical transcription.

## Practical implications for this repo

- It is honest to call the loaf "Georgian-style" rather than "authentic
  Georgian."
- A sponge is worth using because it fits the older bread-making pattern and is
  easy to execute with instant yeast.
- A wholemeal loaf should not be mixed as if it were a dry white boule; the
  dough should be moderately well hydrated and allowed time to absorb water.
- The Dutch oven is a modern substitution for a hotter enclosed bake and fits
  the house workflow.
- Because the loaf is untested in this kitchen, the first version should be
  marked as a trial recipe.

## Inference notes

These parts are modern inferences rather than direct historical instructions:

- using 750 g total flour as the default batch size,
- using the Dutch oven,
- starting around 78% to 80% hydration,
- using fold-based dough development rather than hard kneading throughout,
- choosing instant dry yeast as the leaven.

## Suggested next experiments

- Compare this loaf against a version with 10% to 20% strong white flour.
- Compare a same-day sponge loaf against an overnight sponge.
- Try a second historical loaf later using rye or maslin-style flour blending.
