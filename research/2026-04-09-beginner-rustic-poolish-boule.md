# Research Note: Beginner Rustic Poolish Boule

## Question

How should the user-supplied `ingest.md` notes for a very simple modern rustic
bread be turned into a canonical beginner loaf for this repo?

## Short answer

The strongest route is to keep the core identity:

- poolish,
- mostly white flour,
- bowl mixing and folds,
- banneton proof,
- Dutch-oven bake,

but standardize around the current written Brian Lagerstrom formula rather than
the much wetter transcribed variation in `ingest.md`.

## Source comparison

The ingest notes preserve a formula associated with the linked YouTube demo:

- poolish: `150 g` flour + `150 g` water
- final dough: `280 g` water + `350 g` white flour + `50 g` whole wheat flour

That yields a total dough hydration of about `78%`, which is quite wet for an
absolute beginner boule.

Brian Lagerstrom's current written recipe for `The Easiest Actually Good Bread
(1 Dough 3 Loaves)` keeps the same overall idea but uses:

- poolish: `150 g` flour + `150 g` water
- final dough: `250 g` water + `400 g` white flour + `50 g` whole wheat flour

That produces a total hydration of about `66.7%`, which is much easier to mix,
shape, and score while still making a strong rustic loaf.

## House conclusion

For a recipe whose job is to be:

- simple,
- very good,
- and genuinely beginner friendly,

the current written formula is the better default starting point.

The house recipe therefore keeps:

- the poolish,
- the mostly white-flour profile,
- the fold-built strength,
- the banneton,
- and the Dutch oven,

but avoids the extra-wet beginner path as the mainline version.

## Why not use the wetter formula as the default?

Because the ingest itself already hints at the problem: it warns the dough is
sticky and suggests reducing water by `75 g` if the baker is inexperienced.

That is a sign that the transcribed version is really two recipes hiding inside
one note:

- a wetter, more advanced handling version,
- and an easier fallback version.

For the repo, it is cleaner to define one clear base recipe first, then test
hydration increases later in a controlled way.

## Practical guidance

- Standard white flour is a better fit than the `21%` high-gluten flour.
- Liberal bench and banneton dusting suits this loaf and reduces stress.
- A bold single slash or simple cross is enough; this is not a scoring-showcase
  bread.
- Once the base version is baked successfully, a small water increase of
  `10 g` to `20 g` is the right next experiment if a looser crumb is wanted.

## Suggested next experiments

- Trial the same loaf with `10 g` to `20 g` more water.
- Compare a room-temperature final proof against a short overnight cold proof.
- Decide whether the loaf is best kept at `600 g` total flour or later scaled
  up toward the house `750 g` standard.
