# Research Note: Modern Skillet Focaccia

## Question

How should the repo add a modern focaccia recipe that:

- fits the `10"` lidded cast-iron skillet,
- uses dried herbs and cheese,
- and works cleanly in the current fan-forced oven?

## Short answer

The strongest route is to build from a recent fold-developed high-hydration
focaccia dough, size it for a `10"` cast-iron skillet, and use the skillet lid
for the early bake as a house adaptation.

The topping direction should be simple:

- olive oil,
- dried oregano or similar herbs,
- and grated hard or semi-hard cheese.

## Dough direction

King Arthur's `Big and Bubbly Focaccia` is the clearest current template for
the dough architecture. It uses a wet dough, several bowl folds instead of
hard kneading, a pan rise, dimpling, then a hot bake. It also explicitly says
the formula can be baked in a `10"` cast-iron skillet, where the focaccia will
be slightly taller than in the square pan.

That makes it the best foundation for a skillet-specific house version.

## Flavor direction

King Arthur's `Provolone Oregano Focaccia` provides the most useful flavor
signal for the user's brief: cheese plus herbs, with dried oregano explicitly
accepted when fresh herbs are unavailable.

For the repo's first house version, it is better to keep the flavoring on top
of the dough rather than heavily inside it. That preserves the classic focaccia
look and makes the bread easier to judge after the first bake.

## Cast-iron direction

Lodge's recent cast-iron focaccia recipe reinforces two points:

- cast iron suits focaccia very well because it promotes crisp edges and a
  tender interior,
- and a generous amount of olive oil in the pan is part of what makes the style
  work.

Lodge does not use a lid. The covered first phase in the house recipe is a
modern adaptation to make use of the user's close-fitting skillet lid. This is
an inference from the equipment on hand, not something directly required by the
source recipes.

## House conclusion

The house focaccia should:

1. use a King Arthur style fold-built dough,
2. be sized for the `10"` skillet,
3. use olive oil generously in the pan,
4. top with dried herbs and grated cheese,
5. and use the skillet lid for the first part of the bake, then finish
   uncovered for colour and crispness.

## Suggested next experiments

- Compare covered-then-uncovered baking against uncovered baking for the full
  run.
- Trial a slightly lower-hydration version if the dough proves difficult to
  handle in the skillet.
- Decide whether the cheese is best as parmesan-style, cheddar-style, or a
  blend.
