# Research Note: Scalded Modern Rye Boule

## Question

How should the user-supplied `ingest.md` notes from Baker Bry's rye-scald
technique video be turned into a dependable modern rye loaf for this repo?

## Short answer

Keep the video's core lesson:

- rye benefits from a proper hot scald,
- the dough should be handled more gently than wheat dough,
- and the finished loaf should be cooled for much longer than an ordinary white
  boule,

but adapt the sample loaf into a more dependable house formula by:

- scaling to `750 g` total flour,
- using instant yeast as the default leavening,
- moderating the rye to `40%` rather than copying a full `50/50` yeasted loaf,
- and splitting the wheat portion between plain white flour and some of the
  `21%` Vetta flour so the loaf gets stronger support without becoming a
  high-protein novelty loaf.

## What the video contributes

The ingest notes preserve the main practical message of Baker Bry's video,
`Make Better Rye Bread: One Step You Can't Skip! | Technique Video`, and also
capture his sample formula:

- `300 g` rye flour scalded with `510 g` near-boiling water,
- `300 g` wheat flour,
- `12 g` salt,
- `120 g` sourdough starter or `5 g` yeast,
- and `2 tbsp` honey.

That sample is less a canonical recipe than a demonstration of technique:

- hot rye scald,
- gentle handling,
- restrained expectations about dough feel,
- and longer curing before slicing.

That is useful, but it still needs house adaptation.

## Rye-specific constraints

The main technical issue is that rye is not wheat with stronger flavour.

The American Society of Baking notes that rye's breadmaking performance is
markedly lower than wheat's because it does not build the same continuous
gluten network. They also note that rye dough is typically sticky and difficult
to handle, and that some rye formulas use a `50/50` rye-wheat blend to help
increase dough strength.

King Arthur's professional preferment reference adds an important caution:
when the bread contains a high proportion of rye flour, the rye normally wants
acidification in order to stabilize baking performance and reduce gummy crumb.

That does not mean a straight-yeasted mixed rye loaf is impossible. It does
mean the house recipe should not blindly assume the video's `50/50` sample is
the best default for a commercial-yeast boule in this kitchen.

## House adaptation choice

The cleanest modern house version is therefore:

- `300 g` wholemeal rye flour (`40%` of total flour), all in the scald,
- `300 g` standard white flour,
- `150 g` Vetta `21%` flour,
- instant dry yeast as the base leavening,
- and a Dutch-oven boule format.

This is an inference from the sources, not something any one source states
directly.

Why this split?

- The `40%` rye level still gives the loaf a real rye identity.
- It keeps the dough in a range that is more forgiving for an instant-yeasted
  modern boule.
- Using `150 g` of Vetta and `300 g` of standard white flour makes the wheat
  side behave more like a strong bread flour, without making the whole loaf
  feel artificially high-gluten.

In effect, the wheat portion averages out to something much closer to a strong
support flour than to either of the user's white flours on their own.

## Why keep the hot rye scald?

Because this really is the technique worth keeping from the video.

ASBE's starch-gelatinization reference explains that starch granules swell and
become functional in hot water, forming a paste or gel, and lists rye starch
gelatinization in the `51 C to 60 C` range. DIOSNA's Aroma Ecoline material
likewise treats scalding and warm-held aroma pieces as established thermally
guided pre-doughs for grain products.

For a mixed rye loaf in a home kitchen, that means the scald is doing several
useful jobs at once:

- pre-hydrating thirsty rye flour,
- gelatinizing part of the starch,
- making the dough more moisture-retentive,
- and building a fuller rye flavour.

## Cooling and curing

This is another point where the video and the formal references align well.

King Arthur explicitly notes that dense rye breads often benefit from much
longer cooling than ordinary loaves, in some cases up to `24` hours, to avoid
gummy slicing.

So the house recipe should say plainly:

- cool fully,
- then give the loaf several more hours,
- and preferably leave it until later the same day or next morning before
  slicing.

## House conclusion

The right recipe for this repo is not a literal copy of the video sample.

It should be a `modern scalded rye boule` that:

- keeps the hot rye scald,
- uses instant dry yeast by default,
- stays clearly rye-forward without pushing rye so high that the loaf becomes
  temperamental,
- uses a mixed white-flour support system,
- and bakes in the Dutch oven at the repo's normal hearth-loaf scale.

- Compare this `40%` rye yeasted house loaf against a truer `50/50` sourdough
  version later.
- Decide whether the wheat side is happiest with `150 g` or `200 g` of Vetta.
- Trial a slightly warmer bulk temperature or a cold final retard as the
  default cool-season schedule for this loaf.
