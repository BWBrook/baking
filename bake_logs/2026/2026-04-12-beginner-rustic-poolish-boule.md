# 2026-04-12 Beginner Rustic Poolish Boule

## What was made

A first trial of the `Beginner Rustic Poolish Boule`, baked as a Dutch-oven
boule and cooled fully before slicing.

## Starting point

- Recipe: `recipes/breads/modern/beginner-rustic-poolish-boule.md`
- Poolish target: `150 g` flour + `150 g` water + tiny pinch of yeast
- Final-dough water in recipe: `250 g`
- Kitchen conditions: cool all day, about `15 C`

## What changed this time

- Added an extra `20 g` water during initial mixing because the dough felt a
  little dry after the first squeeze-and-fold stage.
- Left the poolish for about `16` hours in a `12 to 15 C` kitchen.

## Observations during mixing and dough development

- The dough initially felt a little dry and benefited from the extra `20 g`
  water.
- After the extra water, the dough came together well and remained manageable.
- The poolish had matured nicely despite the cool room: bubbly, lively, and
  clearly yeasty-smelling by mixing time.

## Observations during bulk fermentation or resting

- The cool kitchen likely slowed fermentation more than the clock suggested.
- Dough activity was good enough to continue, but in retrospect bulk may have
  needed more time to fully expand the crumb.

## Observations during shaping and final proof

- The dough handled well enough to shape into a neat boule.
- The loaf rose nicely overall, but the final proof was probably still a little
  short for the temperature conditions.

## Observations during baking

- Good oven spring.
- Strong score opening and a handsome, well-coloured crust.
- Finished loaf looked successful and well-shaped rather than flat or dense.

## Outcome

- Crumb was tighter than the recipe target, but not gummy or failed.
- Exterior appearance was excellent, with clear bloom and an attractive rustic
  profile.
- Overall result was a success and a straightforward loaf to make.

## Human judgement

This was a worthwhile first bake of the recipe. The strongest evidence points
to mild under-fermentation from the cool `~15 C` kitchen rather than a problem
with the formula itself. The extra `20 g` water also seemed justified.

## Changes for next time

- Keep the poolish window around `16` hours in cool weather, since that worked
  very well.
- Start with the same formula but allow longer bulk fermentation and final
  proof if the kitchen is again around `15 C`.
- In colder months, trial a Saturday-night final retard and Sunday-morning
  bake-from-cold instead of trying to complete the whole proof at room
  temperature.
- Consider baking this loaf again with the extra `20 g` water built in, then
  compare crumb openness directly against this bake.
