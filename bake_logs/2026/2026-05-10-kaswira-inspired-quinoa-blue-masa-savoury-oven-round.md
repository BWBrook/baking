# 2026-05-10 Kaswira-Inspired Quinoa and Blue Masa Savoury Oven Round

## What was made

A second trial of the oven-baked single-round quinoa and blue masa skillet
bread, adjusted after the 2026-05-09 version came out pasty in the centre.

## Starting point

- Recipe version: second-pass adjustment from the 2026-05-09 trial
- Batch size: about `250 g` total flour
- Planned shape: one cast-iron skillet round
- Planned baking method: oven-baked, with a firm set rather than soft griddle
  texture

## What changed this time

- Held back about `15 ml` water compared with the first trial.
- Changed the flour ratio to about `2/3` quinoa flour and `1/3` blue masa
  harina.
- Added mixed herbs and a little ground pepper.
- Baked the round harder, aiming for a fully set interior.

## Observations during mixing and dough development

- The reduced water made the dough drier and more crumbly.
- Despite being drier, it was still relatively easy to handle.

## Observations during bulk fermentation or resting

- Not applicable: unleavened bread.
- The dough remained workable after hydration even with the reduced water.

## Observations during shaping and final proof

- The single round remained a practical format.
- The drier dough was still pressable into the skillet.

## Observations during baking

- Baking harder set the centre much more successfully than the first trial.
- Final texture moved away from soft bread and toward a savoury biscuit.

## Outcome

- Crumb: dry enough to be fully set, more crumbly than the first version.
- Crust: well baked.
- Flavor: very good; herbs and pepper made a flavourful savoury alternative.
- Structure: held together but ate more like a traditional savoury biscuit
  than a bread.
- Overall success: successful and worth repeating.

## Human judgement

This is a good direction for future trials: less water, a `2:1` quinoa-to-blue-
masa balance, savoury seasoning, and a firmer bake.

## Changes for next time

- Treat this as a deliberate biscuit-like savoury skillet bread rather than a
  failed soft flatbread.
- Start with the lower water range and add water only if the dough is too
  crumbly to press.
- Keep mixed herbs and pepper as a recommended savoury option.
