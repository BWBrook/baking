# Kulluoba Bronze Age Ritual Loaf

## Goal

Make a practical home-kitchen homage to the newly published Kulluoba bread:
small, oval, wholegrain, lightly risen, and compact rather than modern and
voluminous.

## Historical basis

- The Kulluoba bread was found in an Early Bronze Age ritual context in
  Anatolia, near a doorway and deliberately carbonized and buried.
- It measured about `12 cm` across and `2.5 cm` thick.
- Archaeometric analysis indicates that `emmer wheat` was the principal grain,
  with `lentil` present in small amounts.
- The bread also retained coarse particles and a rachis fragment, suggesting
  relatively unsieved grain.
- Irregular air voids indicate kneading and possible light fermentation or
  leavening.
- The preserved starch suggests the loaf may have been baked briefly at high
  temperature.

See `research/2026-04-11-kulluoba-bronze-age-loaf.md` for the supporting note.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Uses wholemeal spelt as the current pantry stand-in for emmer.
- Uses wholemeal bread wheat as a practical support flour.
- Keeps lentil modest so it remains a real but secondary component.
- Uses only a tiny amount of instant yeast and a long cool bulk, because the
  paper supports possible fermentation but not a specific starter system.
- Uses the cast-iron skillet and oven as the best current analogue for a hot
  early bake environment.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wholemeal spelt flour | 150 g | 61.2 | Emmer stand-in |
| Plain wholemeal bread wheat flour | 80 g | 32.7 | Practical support flour |
| Ground dried lentils | 15 g | 6.1 | Brown or green lentils, ground fairly fine |
| Water | 175 to 185 g | 71.4 to 75.5 | Start with `175 g` |
| Fine salt | 4.5 g | 1.8 |  |
| Instant dry yeast | 0.4 g | 0.16 | A scant `1/8 tsp`, or a small pinch |

## Method

1. If needed, grind dried lentils to a fairly fine meal. A little unevenness is
   acceptable; the loaf should not feel hyper-refined.
2. In a bowl mix the spelt flour, wholemeal wheat flour, ground lentils, salt,
   and yeast.
3. Add `175 g` water and mix until a rough dough forms.
4. Rest `20` minutes so the wholegrain flours and lentil can hydrate.
5. Knead briefly, just until the dough becomes cohesive and moderately smooth.
   Do not chase a strong modern windowpane.
6. If the dough feels cramped or visibly dry, add a little more water. The
   dough should be firm but pliable.
7. Cover and bulk ferment for `8 to 12` hours in a cool room. Give it one fold
   halfway through if convenient.
8. Turn the dough out gently and shape it into a compact oval disk about `12 to
   14 cm` across and `2.5 to 3 cm` thick.
9. Rest or proof for `20 to 35` minutes only. The loaf should feel a little
   aerated, not swollen.
10. While it rests, preheat the cast-iron skillet in the oven at `240 C`
    fan-forced for `30` minutes. Keep the skillet dry or only very lightly
    dusted.
11. Transfer the loaf carefully to the hot skillet. If you want easier loading,
    use a small piece of baking paper trimmed close to the dough.
12. Bake covered for `10` minutes at `230 C` fan-forced.
13. Remove the lid, reduce to `210 C` fan-forced, and bake for a further `10
    to 14` minutes, until well coloured and clearly set.
14. Cool fully before cutting. Expect a compact loaf rather than a lofty one.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `245 g`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated cast-iron skillet with lid
- Preheat: `240 C` fan-forced for `30` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `230 C` covered, then `210 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `10` minutes covered, `10 to 14` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: compact, lightly aerated, and grainy rather than fluffy
- Crust: firm, darkened, and slightly hearth-like
- Flavor: nutty wholegrain, mildly earthy, with a faint legume note
- Shape: small thick oval loaf with restrained rise

## Notes

- This is a modern homage, not a molecule-for-molecule reconstruction.
- Spelt is only a practical stand-in for emmer until true emmer flour is
  acquired.
- The loaf should feel more like a dense ritual hearth bread than a modern
  artisan boule.
- If the first version rises more than you want, shorten the final proof before
  reducing the yeast.

## Historical serving note

- This loaf makes sense torn, broken, or sliced thickly rather than treated as
  a sandwich bread.
- It should pair well with olive oil, cheese, pulses, or a simple stew.
