# Late Saxon Ipswich-Style Mixed Wheat Loaf

## Goal

Make a Saxon-style loaf that captures the spirit of the surviving Ipswich
carbonized loaves: mixed-grain, darker than a white loaf, and recognizably
English in character rather than Scandinavian or later Georgian.

## Historical basis

- Ipswich Museum states that the surviving Anglo-Saxon loaves from the
  Buttermarket excavations were made from a mixture of rye, emmer wheat, and
  bread or club wheat.
- Wider archaeobotanical evidence for Saxon England consistently includes bread
  wheat, barley, oats, and rye.
- Post-Roman Britain saw a shift away from spelt toward bread wheat, rye,
  barley, and oats as the key bread cereals.

See `research/2026-04-07-viking-and-saxon-breads.md` for the source note.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Uses standard white wheat flour as the stand-in for bread or club wheat.
- Uses wholemeal spelt as the stand-in for emmer, because emmer is not yet in
  the pantry.
- Uses wholemeal rye to keep the historical grain mix visible.
- Uses a simple overnight sponge with a little yeast to give some maturity and
  flavour without requiring a live barm or sourdough culture.
- Uses the Dutch oven as the closest current stand-in for a small enclosed oven
  bake in this kitchen.

## Total formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 450 g | 60 | Bread or club wheat stand-in |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 200 g | 26.7 | Historical grain signal |
| Wholemeal spelt flour | 100 g | 13.3 | Emmer stand-in |
| Water | 510 to 530 g | 68 to 70.7 | Includes sponge water |
| Fine salt | 14 g | 1.9 |  |
| Instant dry yeast | 4 g | 0.5 | Includes sponge yeast |

## Sponge

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 150 g |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 100 g |
| Water | 250 g |
| Instant dry yeast | 1 g |

Mix, cover, and leave for `8 to 12` hours at cool room temperature.

## Final dough additions

| Ingredient | Amount | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 300 g | 40 | Added to sponge |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 100 g | 13.3 | Added to sponge |
| Wholemeal spelt flour | 100 g | 13.3 | Emmer stand-in |
| Water | 260 to 280 g | 34.7 to 37.3 | Start with `260 g` |
| Fine salt | 14 g | 1.9 |  |
| Instant dry yeast | 3 g | 0.4 | Added to final dough |

## Method

1. Make the sponge the night before. It should be bubbly and clearly expanded
   by the next day.
2. Add the final-dough flours, salt, yeast, and `260 g` water to the sponge.
3. Mix until a cohesive dough forms. Add a little more water only if needed.
   The dough should be firm enough to hold shape, but not dry or cramped.
4. Knead for about `8` minutes by hand or until the dough is reasonably smooth.
   It will not become as silky as a pure white dough.
5. Bulk ferment for `1 1/2 to 2` hours, with one fold halfway through if the
   dough seems slack.
6. Shape into a round and place seam-side up in a floured banneton or a cloth-
   lined bowl.
7. Final-proof for `45 to 75` minutes, until the dough is lightly puffy but not
   overblown.
8. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `240 C` fan-forced for `40 to 45`
   minutes.
9. Turn out the loaf, score a simple cross or single slash, and load into the
   Dutch oven.
10. Bake covered for `22` minutes.
11. Remove the lid, reduce the oven to `220 C` fan-forced, and bake for a
    further `20 to 24` minutes.
12. Cool fully before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `750 g`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `240 C` fan-forced for `40 to 45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `240 C` covered, then `220 C` uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: moderately close, slightly chewy, and hearty
- Crust: dark and robust rather than thin and delicate
- Flavor: wheaty first, then rye depth and a mild old-grain note from the
  spelt
- Shape: firm rounded loaf with a controlled rustic bloom

## Notes

- This is a modern adaptation of the Ipswich grain profile, not a literal
  reconstruction.
- If you later acquire emmer, replace the spelt directly and reassess
  hydration.
- A sourdough or barm version may prove even more convincing later, but this is
  the best manageable first house version.
