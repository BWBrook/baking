# Viking Barley, Oat, and Rye Hearth Bread

## Goal

Make a coarse, grain-forward Viking-style bread that feels plausible for an
everyday Scandinavian hearth or pan context and is fully manageable in a modern
kitchen.

## Historical basis

- A Viking grave bread from Knivberga is described by the Swedish History
  Museum as pieces of a thick, round, flat loaf probably made with barley and
  oats and baked on a frying pan or bakestone.
- Archaeobotanical evidence from Viking-period east-central Sweden shows bread
  wheat, rye, oats, and hulled barley as the key cereal species.
- The richer wheat, honey, and butter `Birka bread` published by the Viking
  Ship Museum is best treated as a finer bread or biscuit, not as the common
  everyday representative loaf.

See `research/2026-04-07-viking-and-saxon-breads.md` for the source note.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Keeps the bread low-profile and hearth-like rather than making a modern
  risen loaf.
- Uses barley and oats as the main historical signal.
- Adds a moderate amount of wholemeal rye as a practical support flour, because
  a purely barley-and-oat dough is much harder to handle well in a modern home
  kitchen.
- Uses the cast-iron skillet as the bakestone equivalent.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barley flour | 180 g | Main flour |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 100 g | Support flour |
| Oat flour | 70 g | Historical flavour and softness |
| Fine salt | 6 g |  |
| Hot water | 250 to 265 g | Start with `250 g` |

## Method

1. In a bowl mix the barley flour, rye flour, oat flour, and salt.
2. Add `250 g` hot water and stir until a stiff, slightly tacky dough forms.
3. Rest `15` minutes so the grain flours fully hydrate.
4. If the dough is still too dry to press together cleanly, add a little more
   water. It should feel firm, not slack.
5. Divide into `4` pieces.
6. Press each piece into a round about `10 to 12 cm` wide and roughly `8 to
   10 mm` thick. Use damp or lightly floured hands if needed.
7. Heat the cast-iron skillet over medium to medium-low heat. Keep it dry, or
   wipe with the thinnest possible film of oil if sticking is a concern.
8. Cook the breads in batches for about `4 to 5` minutes on the first side and
   `3 to 4` minutes on the second, adjusting the heat so they cook through
   without scorching.
9. If the breads are very thick, cover the skillet for part of the cooking to
   help the centre set.
10. Cool briefly on a cloth before serving warm.

## Cooking setup

- Vessel: cast-iron skillet
- Heat source: stovetop
- Heat level: medium to medium-low
- Time: about `7 to 9` minutes per bread

## Result targets

- Texture: dense, hearty, and slightly chewy rather than fluffy
- Flavor: nutty, earthy, and rustic
- Structure: firm enough to tear, dip, or split, but not light like modern
  flatbread
- Appearance: browned patches from the skillet, with a darker grain-speckled
  crumb

## Notes

- This is an intentionally plain bread.
- If the first batch feels too crumbly, make the next breads a touch thicker or
  add a little extra hot water to the remaining dough.
- Best served warm with butter, cheese, fish, stew, or soup.
