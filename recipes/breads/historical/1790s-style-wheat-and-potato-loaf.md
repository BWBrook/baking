# 1790s-Style Wheat-and-Potato Loaf

## Goal

Make a British shortage-era wheat-and-potato loaf in the spirit of the late
1790s and early 1800s, using prepared plain `Deb` mash as a practical stand-in
for boiled, sieved potato in a modern home kitchen.

## Historical basis

- During the scarcity years of `1795-1796`, the Board of Agriculture was
  actively asked about the right proportion of flour to potatoes in bread and
  collected information on mixed potato breads.
- By `1805/1806`, Alexander Hunter's printed `potato bread` formula used fine
  flour rubbed into warm mealy potatoes, then yeast, salt, milk, and water,
  with the loaf baked in a tin.
- The historical purpose was partly economy and scarcity substitution, but
  potato bread was also valued for lightness and keeping quality.

See `research/2026-04-07-potato-bread-and-deb-flakes.md` for the source note
behind this recipe.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Uses prepared plain `Deb` mash as the nearest practical stand-in for cooked,
  sieved potato.
- Uses standard white flour rather than the 21% high-gluten flour, because the
  historical idea is a light wheat loaf rather than a chewy modern bread.
- Uses instant dry yeast instead of barm or older yeast methods.
- Keeps milk and water together in the dough because that aligns well with the
  early printed English potato-bread pattern.
- Bakes in the Dutch oven because there is no loaf tin yet, even though the
  historical printed source points toward a tin loaf.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 500 g | Main wheat flour |
| Prepared plain Deb mash | 250 g | Make according to the packet ratio; aim for a firm plain mash |
| Milk | 120 g | Lukewarm |
| Water | 110 to 130 g | Start with `110 g`, then adjust |
| Fine salt | 7 g | Reduced because Deb already contains salt |
| Instant dry yeast | 5 g |  |
| Rice flour or flour for dusting | as needed | For banneton release |

## Prepared mash

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Deb instant mashed potato | 52 g |
| Boiling water | Enough to make about `250 g` firm plain mash by the packet ratio |

## Method

1. Make the plain mash first. Mix the `Deb` with the boiling water until
   smooth, then let it cool to lukewarm. Do not add milk, butter, or pepper.
2. In a large bowl combine the flour, salt, and yeast.
3. Add the prepared mash, the milk, and `110 g` water. Mix until all the flour
   is hydrated.
4. Rest the dough `10 to 15` minutes so the flour and potato can absorb the
   liquid.
5. Reassess hydration. If the dough feels too stiff to knead smoothly, add some
   or all of the remaining `20 g` water.
6. Knead for about `8 to 10` minutes until the dough is smooth, elastic, and
   clearly more cohesive than when first mixed.
7. Return to a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and bulk ferment until well risen and
   lively, usually `60 to 90` minutes.
8. Turn the dough out, pre-shape gently into a round, and rest `10` minutes.
9. Final-shape into a boule and place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton.
10. Final-proof until the dough springs back slowly when pressed, usually `40`
    to `60` minutes.
11. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `240 C` fan-forced for `30 to 45`
    minutes.
12. Turn the loaf out, score simply, and load into the Dutch oven.
13. Bake covered for `20` minutes at `230 C` fan-forced.
14. Remove the lid, reduce to `210 C` fan-forced, and bake for a further `20 to
    25` minutes until the loaf is golden-brown and about `95 to 96 C`
    internally.
15. Cool fully before slicing. This loaf is especially worth trying toasted.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `500 g` wheat flour plus potato substitution
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `240 C` fan-forced for `30 to 45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `230 C` covered, then `210 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `20` minutes covered, `20 to 25` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: soft, even, and slightly creamy rather than dry
- Crust: golden and moderately crisp
- Flavor: mild wheat bread with a gentle potato sweetness
- Shape: smaller round loaf with respectable lift and good toasting quality

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough wants the full extra `20 g` water
- Whether the reduced salt is still sufficient with this specific Deb product
- Whether the loaf prefers a slightly shorter final proof
- Whether the Dutch oven gives enough support for this softer dough

## Notes

- This is a historical-style adaptation, not a strict reconstruction.
- `Deb` is only a partial stand-in for plain cooked potato: it also contains
  salt, dairy ingredients, oil, flavourings, and preservative.
- Prepared mash is a better historical stand-in than adding dry flakes
  directly.
