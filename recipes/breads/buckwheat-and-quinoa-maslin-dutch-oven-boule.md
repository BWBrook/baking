# Buckwheat and Quinoa Maslin Dutch-Oven Boule

## Goal

Make a yeasted mixed-flour loaf in the Dutch oven using strong white bread
flour as the structural majority, with buckwheat and quinoa contributing
flavor and character without collapsing the loaf.

## Source basis

This house recipe is informed by:

- Bob's Red Mill guidance on practical substitution ranges for barley,
  buckwheat, and quinoa,
- Bob's Red Mill yeast-bread recipes using quinoa flour and buckwheat flour,
- King Arthur's testing guidance that yeast breads become dense and fractured
  when ancient-grain substitutions rise too high,
- and the current house Dutch-oven workflow.

See `research/2026-04-07-barley-buckwheat-quinoa-breads.md` for the supporting
note.

## Blend logic

The house default blend is:

- `73%` strong white bread flour,
- `17%` buckwheat flour,
- `10%` quinoa flour.

Reason:

- the wheat stays dominant enough to build a real loaf,
- buckwheat adds earthy depth without pushing too far into dense territory,
- quinoa stays present but controlled.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Grams | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High-gluten white bread flour | 550 | 73.3 | Structural majority |
| Buckwheat flour | 125 | 16.7 | Earthy accent flour |
| Quinoa flour | 75 | 10 | Nutty accent flour |
| Water | 535 to 550 | 71.3 to 73.3 | Start with `535 g`, hold back `15 g` |
| Fine salt | 15 | 2 |  |
| Instant dry yeast | 4 to 6 | 0.5 to 0.8 | Use `4 g` for overnight cold proof, `6 g` for same-day |
| Honey | 15 | 2 | Optional but recommended |
| Olive oil | 15 | 2 | Optional but recommended |
| Rice flour or flour for dusting | as needed | For banneton release |

## Method

1. In a large bowl combine all the flours and `520 g` of the water. Mix until
   no dry flour remains. Cover and rest `30 to 45` minutes.
2. Add the salt, yeast, honey, olive oil, and the remaining `15 g` water if
   needed. Mix until the dough becomes cohesive and elastic. It will not feel
   as silky as an all-wheat dough, but it should strengthen clearly.
3. Bulk ferment for about `2 1/2 to 3 1/2` hours at room temperature, giving
   `3` or `4` sets of stretch-and-folds during the first `90` minutes.
4. When the dough is puffy, aerated, and roughly `60%` to `80%` larger, turn
   it onto a lightly floured bench and pre-shape into a round.
5. Rest `10` minutes, then final-shape into a taut boule.
6. Place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton.
7. Final-proof either:
   `Same day:` `45 to 75` minutes at room temperature.
   `Cold proof:` `8 to 16` hours in the fridge for better flavor and handling.
8. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `250 C` fan-forced for `45` minutes.
9. Turn the loaf out, score with one bold slash, and load into the Dutch oven.
10. Bake covered for `20` minutes at `240 C` fan-forced.
11. Remove the lid, reduce to `220 C` fan-forced, and bake for a further `20 to
    25` minutes until deep brown and about `96 to 98 C` internally.
12. Cool at least `1` hour before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `750 g`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `250 C` fan-forced for `45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `240 C` covered, then `220 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `20` minutes covered, `20 to 25` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: moderately open for a mixed-flour loaf, not sandy or gummy
- Crust: dark, robust, and well coloured
- Flavor: wheaty first, then earthy buckwheat and nutty quinoa
- Shape: strong rounded boule with controlled bloom

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough prefers the full `550 g` water
- Whether your preferred balance is the `4 g` cold-proof route or the `6 g`
  same-day route
- Whether the honey and oil are worth keeping in the default version
- Whether the loaf wants a slightly shorter uncovered bake in your oven

## Notes

- The strong white flour is doing essential structural work here.
- If the crumb feels crumbly, raise hydration before you raise the non-wheat
  flour percentages.
- If the loaf feels gummy, bake slightly longer with the lid off before
  reducing quinoa.
