# Beginner Rustic Poolish Boule

## Goal

Make a simple but excellent modern beginner loaf: poolish-led, mostly white
flour, bowl-mixed, banneton-proofed, and baked in the Dutch oven for strong
lift and a dark crust.

## Source basis

This house recipe is informed by:

- the user-supplied ingest notes from the linked YouTube demo,
- Brian Lagerstrom's current written version of the same beginner rustic-bread
  idea,
- and the repo's house Dutch-oven workflow.

See `research/2026-04-09-beginner-rustic-poolish-boule.md` for the supporting
note.

## House choices

- Uses the standard white flour rather than the `21%` high-gluten flour. This
  is a better fit for a beginner rustic loaf.
- Keeps a poolish because it gives a lot of flavour return for very little
  extra work.
- Uses a smaller dough than the repo's usual `750 g` flour format, because
  this recipe is meant to be a true beginner's bread rather than a house-scale
  default.
- Standardizes around the easier, more dependable current written formula
  rather than the wetter `78%` hydration transcription in `ingest.md`.
- Keeps the banneton and Dutch oven because they are already normal parts of
  this kitchen.

## Poolish

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 150 g |
| Water | 150 g |
| Instant dry yeast | A tiny pinch |

Mix, cover, and leave at room temperature for `12 to 24` hours. `16` hours is
an especially good target.

## Final dough

| Ingredient | Amount | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 400 g | 66.7 | Added to poolish |
| Plain wholemeal bread wheat flour | 50 g | 8.3 | Added to poolish |
| Water | 250 g | 41.7 | Warm, about `30 C` |
| Fine salt | 12 g | 2 |  |
| Instant dry yeast | 2 g | 0.3 |  |
| All ripe poolish | 300 g | Includes `150 g` flour + `150 g` water |
| Rice flour or plain flour | as needed | For banneton dusting |

## Method

1. Make the poolish the day before baking. By the time it is ready it should
   look expanded, bubbly, and lively.
2. In a large bowl combine the warm water, yeast, and all of the ripe poolish.
3. Add the white flour, wholemeal flour, and salt.
4. Stir with a sturdy spoon until the dough is roughly combined, then use a wet
   hand to squeeze and fold the dough until no dry flour remains.
5. Cover and rest `20` minutes.
6. With a wet hand, perform one full round of bowl folds, pulling up and over
   around the dough about `8` times. Then round the dough gently in the bowl so
   the top gains some tension.
7. Cover and rest `30` minutes.
8. Repeat the folds and rounding once more.
9. Cover and ferment for a further `45 to 75` minutes, until the dough is
   clearly aerated and roughly doubled.
10. Dust the bench generously with flour and turn out the dough gently.
11. Pre-shape into a loose round and rest `10` minutes.
12. Final-shape into a boule by folding the edges inward, then turning seam-
    side down and tightening the surface with gentle dragging motions.
13. Dust the banneton well, ideally with a mix that includes some rice flour.
    Place the loaf seam-side up in the banneton and dust the exposed surface
    lightly as well.
14. Proof `45 to 75` minutes, until the loaf feels fuller and a floured finger
    poke springs back slowly.
15. While the loaf proofs, preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `245 C`
    fan-forced for `35 to 45` minutes.
16. Turn the loaf out onto parchment or directly into the Dutch oven. Score one
    bold slash or a simple cross.
17. Bake covered at `235 C` fan-forced for `20` minutes.
18. Remove the lid, reduce the oven to `220 C` fan-forced, and bake for a
    further `18 to 22` minutes until the crust is deeply coloured.
19. Cool fully before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `600 g`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `245 C` fan-forced for `35 to 45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `235 C` covered, then `220 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `20` minutes covered, `18 to 22` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: moderately open, glossy, and tender rather than tight
- Crust: dark, crisp, and rustic
- Flavor: clean wheaty flavour with a mild prefermented depth
- Shape: strong rounded loaf with clear oven spring

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough wants the full proof time in your kitchen
- Whether parchment transfer or direct loading feels more reliable
- Whether you prefer a single slash or a cross score on this loaf
- Whether the loaf would benefit from `10 g` to `20 g` extra water once the
  base version is established

## Notes

- Liberal dusting during shaping and banneton loading is part of the style here
  and helps keep the beginner workflow calm.
- If you want a stickier, more open-crumbed version later, increase water only
  modestly at first rather than jumping straight to a very high hydration.
- This is a modern teaching loaf, not a historical bread.
