# Skillet Herb and Cheese Focaccia

## Goal

Make a modern focaccia sized for the `10"` lidded cast-iron skillet: airy,
olive-oil-rich, heavily dimpled, and topped with dried herbs and cheese.

## Source basis

This house recipe is informed by:

- King Arthur's recent tall, fold-built focaccia method,
- King Arthur's herb-and-cheese focaccia flavor logic,
- and Lodge's cast-iron focaccia guidance.

See `research/2026-04-09-modern-skillet-focaccia.md` for the supporting note.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Uses the `10"` cast-iron skillet as the pan rather than a square tin.
- Uses the skillet lid for the early bake to trap some steam and help upward
  lift before browning.
- Keeps the dough as a high-hydration white-flour focaccia rather than turning
  it into a mixed-flour experiment.
- Uses dried herbs, because that is what you specifically asked for and they
  are a practical house pantry item.
- Uses grated hard or semi-hard cheese on top rather than inside the dough, so
  the focaccia stays recognizably open and dimpled.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 360 g | 100 | Main flour |
| Water | 300 g | 83.3 | Warm, about `30 C` |
| Olive oil | 18 g | 5 | In the dough |
| Fine salt | 9 g | 2.5 |  |
| Sugar or honey | 6 g | 1.7 | Helps fermentation and browning |
| Instant dry yeast | 4 g | 1.1 |  |

## Pan and topping

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Olive oil | 30 g | For the skillet and top |
| Dried oregano | 1 tsp | Main herb |
| Dried rosemary or mixed Italian herbs | 1/2 tsp | Optional but recommended |
| Grated hard or semi-hard cheese | 70 to 90 g | Parmesan, pecorino, or cheddar all work |
| Flaky salt | as needed | Optional finishing salt |

## Method

1. In a large bowl whisk together the flour, salt, sugar or honey, and yeast.
2. Add the water and `18 g` olive oil. Mix until no dry patches remain. The
   dough will look shaggy and wet.
3. Cover and rest `15` minutes.
4. Perform a bowl fold: with a wet hand, lift one section of dough and fold it
   back into the center. Rotate the bowl and repeat until you have worked all
   the way around.
5. Rest `15` minutes and repeat the fold.
6. Rest `15` minutes and repeat a third fold.
7. Rest `15` minutes and repeat a fourth and final fold. The dough should now
   feel smoother, stronger, and airier.
8. Cover and let rise for about `1` hour, or until clearly puffy and expanded.
9. Generously oil the bottom and sides of the `10"` cast-iron skillet with
   about `20 g` of the olive oil.
10. Transfer the dough to the skillet. Turn it once so it gets lightly coated
    in oil, then gently stretch and press it outward. It does not need to reach
    the edges immediately.
11. Cover and let rise in the skillet for `45 to 75` minutes, until the dough
    is marshmallowy and has spread close to the sides.
12. Heat the oven to `220 C` fan-forced.
13. Oil your fingertips lightly and dimple the dough deeply all over.
14. Drizzle over the remaining `10 g` olive oil, then scatter the dried herbs
    and grated cheese evenly across the top. Add a little flaky salt if you
    want a stronger finish.
15. Cover the skillet with its lid and bake for `12` minutes on the middle
    shelf.
16. Remove the lid and continue baking for `10 to 14` minutes, until the top
    is deeply golden and the cheese is well coloured.
17. If needed, give the focaccia `1 to 2` minutes under the grill for extra
    browning.
18. Rest in the skillet for `5` minutes, then lift out carefully and cool on a
    rack for at least `10` minutes before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `360 g`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: `10"` lidded cast-iron skillet
- Bake temperatures: `220 C` covered, then `220 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `12` minutes covered, `10 to 14` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: airy, moist, and slightly chewy
- Crust: crisp-edged, olive-oil-fried at the base, and bronzed on top
- Flavor: fragrant with dried herbs, salt, olive oil, and toasted cheese
- Shape: tall round focaccia with distinct deep dimples

## Variables to watch

- Whether your skillet prefers the full `30 g` oil or slightly less
- Whether the dough is happiest closer to `45` or `75` minutes in the skillet
- Whether the lid-covered phase gives enough lift without softening the top too
  much
- Which cheese becomes the house favorite

## Notes

- This is a modern house focaccia, not an attempt at historical Italian
  reconstruction.
- Standard white flour is the right fit here; the very high-gluten flour would
  likely push the bread toward chewiness more than you want.
- If the dough resists stretching in the skillet, wait `10` minutes and try
  again rather than forcing it.
