# Deb Potato-Flake Convenience Loaf

## Goal

Make a practical modern wheat-and-potato loaf using `Deb` flakes directly in
the dough, without first turning them into mash.

## Source basis

This recipe is not historical in process, but it is informed by:

- the same wheat-and-potato loaf tradition behind the historical-style recipe,
- the `Deb` packet hydration ratio,
- modern baking practice where potato flakes are often used directly in yeast
  doughs for softness and keeping quality.

See `research/2026-04-07-potato-bread-and-deb-flakes.md` for the shared source
note.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Uses dry `Deb` flakes directly in the flour mix for convenience.
- Uses the same approximate potato charge as the prepared-mash version:
  `52 g` Deb, which corresponds roughly to `250 g` prepared mash by the packet
  directions.
- Keeps the loaf in the house Dutch-oven workflow.
- Uses standard white flour as the baseline.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 500 g | Main flour |
| Deb instant mashed potato flakes | 52 g | Added dry to the flour |
| Milk | 120 g | Lukewarm |
| Water | 330 to 350 g | Start with `330 g`, then adjust |
| Fine salt | 6 g | Reduced because Deb already contains salt |
| Instant dry yeast | 5 g |  |
| Rice flour or flour for dusting | as needed | For banneton release |

## Method

1. In a large bowl combine the flour, dry `Deb` flakes, salt, and yeast.
2. Add the milk and `330 g` water. Mix until all the dry ingredients are
   hydrated.
3. Rest the dough `15` minutes so the potato flakes can fully absorb liquid.
4. Reassess hydration. If the dough feels too stiff or rough after the rest,
   add more water `10 g` at a time until it becomes soft and kneadable.
5. Knead for about `8 to 10` minutes until smooth and elastic.
6. Return to a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and bulk ferment until well risen and
   lively, usually `60 to 90` minutes.
7. Turn the dough out, pre-shape gently into a round, and rest `10` minutes.
8. Final-shape into a boule and place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton.
9. Final-proof until the dough springs back slowly when pressed, usually `40`
   to `60` minutes.
10. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `240 C` fan-forced for `30 to 45`
    minutes.
11. Turn the loaf out, score simply, and load into the Dutch oven.
12. Bake covered for `20` minutes at `230 C` fan-forced.
13. Remove the lid, reduce to `210 C` fan-forced, and bake for a further `20 to
    25` minutes until the loaf is golden-brown and about `95 to 96 C`
    internally.
14. Cool fully before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `500 g` wheat flour plus direct potato-flake addition
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `240 C` fan-forced for `30 to 45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `230 C` covered, then `210 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `20` minutes covered, `20 to 25` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: soft and moist, with good keeping quality
- Crust: golden and moderately crisp
- Flavor: clean wheat loaf with a subtle potato note
- Shape: round loaf with slightly better structural predictability than the
  prepared-mash version

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough wants the full `350 g` water
- Whether the direct flakes give slightly more predictable handling than mash
- Whether the loaf still needs a little more salt
- Whether the crumb stays soft longer than the prepared-mash version

## Notes

- This is a convenience loaf, not a historical reconstruction.
- Adding the flakes dry makes them behave more like a modern dough improver
  than like true cooked potato.
- If you want the more historically faithful version, use the prepared-mash
  recipe instead.
