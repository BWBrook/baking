# Scalded Honey Rye Boule

## Goal

Make a modern rye-forward loaf with better moisture, fuller flavour, and more
dependable handling by scalding the rye flour first, then supporting it with a
strong white-wheat portion and baking it as a Dutch-oven boule.

## Source basis

This recipe is based on:

- the user-supplied notes from Baker Bry's rye-scald technique video,
- formal rye-handling guidance from the American Society of Baking,
- and house adaptation choices for this kitchen's flour supply and Dutch-oven
  workflow.

See `research/2026-04-13-scalded-modern-rye-boule.md` for the supporting note.

## House choices

- Keeps the hot rye scald as the central technique.
- Scales to the house `750 g` total-flour size.
- Uses instant dry yeast as the mainline version, since that is the current
  household default.
- Moderates the rye to `40%` of total flour rather than copying a straight
  `50/50` yeasted loaf, because higher rye percentages classically want more
  acidity control.
- Splits the wheat portion between `300 g` standard white flour and `150 g`
  Vetta `21%` flour so the support flour behaves more like a strong bread flour
  without making the loaf entirely high-protein.

## Rye scald

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Plain wholemeal rye flour | 300 g |
| Water | 510 g |

Pour the hot water over the rye flour, stir until no dry flour remains, cover,
and let cool until just warm or room temperature. This can be done `2 to 12`
hours ahead.

## Final dough

| Ingredient | Amount | Baker's % | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 300 g | 40 | Main wheat support flour |
| Vetta `21%` white flour | 150 g | 20 | Added strength without going full high-protein |
| Warm water | 30 g | 4 | Add at mix time |
| Extra water | 0 to 30 g | 0 to 4 | Only if dough feels too stiff after initial mix |
| Fine salt | 15 g | 2 |  |
| Instant dry yeast | 6 g | 0.8 |  |
| Honey | 45 g | 6 | Keeps the video's slight sweetness |
| All cooled rye scald | 810 g | Includes `300 g` flour + `510 g` water |
| Rice flour or rye flour | as needed | For banneton dusting |

## Method

1. Make the rye scald. Mix the rye flour with the hot water until smooth and
   fully combined. Cover and cool until lukewarm or room temperature.
2. In a large bowl, combine the cooled rye scald, `30 g` warm water, honey,
   and yeast.
3. Add the standard white flour, Vetta flour, and salt.
4. Mix with a sturdy spoon, dough whisk, or wet hand until no dry flour
   remains. Expect a sticky, pasty dough rather than a classic elastic wheat
   dough.
5. Cover and rest `20` minutes.
6. Reassess hydration. If the dough feels unusually stiff or resistant for a
   rye-heavy dough, add extra water `10 g` to `15 g` at a time, up to `30 g`
   more.
7. With wet hands, perform one gentle round of bowl folds. Pull the dough up
   and over itself about `6` to `8` times. Do not chase full wheat-style
   gluten development.
8. Cover and rest `30` minutes.
9. If the dough still feels loose and under-organized, give it one more gentle
   fold. If it already has some body, leave it alone.
10. Bulk ferment until the dough is clearly aerated and about `50%` to `75%`
    larger. In a warm kitchen this may take around `60` to `90` minutes; in a
    cool `10 C` to `16 C` kitchen it may take noticeably longer.
11. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured bench. Use a scraper and floured
    hands to gather it into a loose round. Rest `10` minutes.
12. Final-shape into a boule with moderate surface tension.
13. Place seam-side up in a well-dusted banneton. Rice flour mixed with a
    little rye flour works especially well.
14. Final-proof either:
    - at room temperature for about `45` to `75` minutes, until the loaf feels
      fuller and a floured finger poke springs back slowly, or
    - in the refrigerator overnight after a short `15` to `20` minute bench
      rest, for a cold-season bake-from-cold workflow.
15. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `240 C` fan-forced for `35` to `45`
    minutes.
16. Turn out the loaf, score one bold slash or a simple cross, and load it into
    the Dutch oven.
17. Bake covered at `230 C` fan-forced for `20` minutes.
18. Remove the lid, reduce to `210 C` fan-forced, and bake for a further `22`
    to `28` minutes, until the crust is deep brown and the internal
    temperature is about `95 C` to `96 C`.
19. Cool fully, then let the loaf cure for at least several more hours before
    slicing. Overnight is ideal if you can wait.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `750 g`
- Rye proportion: `40%`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `240 C` fan-forced for `35` to `45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `230 C` covered, then `210 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `20` minutes covered, `22` to `28` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: even, moist, and sliceable, slightly tighter than a white country loaf
- Crust: dark brown, lightly crackled, and well set
- Flavor: clear rye flavour with a gentle sweetness from honey
- Shape: rounded boule with respectable lift rather than a flat disk

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough wants the extra `10 g` to `30 g` water
- Whether a cold overnight proof gives cleaner scoring and slightly better
  crumb in cool months
- Whether the `300 g` standard white plus `150 g` Vetta split is the best wheat
  support balance
- Whether the loaf benefits from a full overnight cure before first slicing

## Notes

- This loaf should feel stickier and less elastic than a wheat-dominant dough.
- The scald improves the loaf, but it does not make rye behave exactly like
  wheat.
- If you later want a more assertive or more traditional rye loaf, the next
  logical step is a sourdough or acidified-rye version rather than simply
  pushing the rye percentage higher in this same straight-yeasted formula.
