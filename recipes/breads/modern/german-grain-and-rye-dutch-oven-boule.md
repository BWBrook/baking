# German Grain and Rye Dutch-Oven Boule

## Goal

Turn one `600 g` Laucke German Grain premix into a single `750 g` house boule
by adding `150 g` wholemeal rye flour, then baking it in the Dutch oven rather
than as a tin loaf.

## Source basis

This recipe is based on:

- the Laucke product page,
- the packet ingredient panel,
- the packet's hand-kneaded formula and method.

The manufacturer hand-kneaded method for one `600 g` pack is:

- `600 g` bread mix,
- `360 ml` water,
- `2 tsp` yeast,
- vigorous kneading,
- one bulk rise to about double,
- degas, shape into an oiled tin,
- second rise to at least double,
- bake at `220 C conventional / 200 C fan-forced` for `25 to 30` minutes.

This house version keeps the same general system but adapts it to:

- `600 g` mix plus `150 g` wholemeal rye flour,
- one banneton-proofed boule,
- Dutch-oven baking in the fan-forced electric oven,
- more careful hydration control for a shape-holding loaf.

## Mix notes

The premix already includes:

- unbleached wheat flour,
- grain inclusions (`7%`) including kibbled rye, linseed, rolled wheat, maize
  polenta, and wheat bran,
- non-iodised salt,
- malt flour,
- emulsifier (`E481`),
- inactive dry yeast,
- ascorbic acid,
- enzyme,
- added vitamins.

The yeast sachet includes:

- dry yeast,
- emulsifier (`E491`).

That means this is a grain bread premix with its own salt and improvers
already built in. The added rye should reinforce the bread's intended profile
better than spelt would, because the mix already leans toward a dark, grainy,
rye-forward character.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laucke German Grain Bread Mix | 600 g | One full internal flour bag |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 150 g | House addition |
| Instant dry yeast | 2 tsp | Or use the included yeast sachet for one `600 g` pack |
| Water | 450 to 470 g | Start at `450 g`, then adjust carefully |
| Rice flour or flour for dusting | as needed | Best for banneton release |

## Hydration guidance

The manufacturer uses `360 ml` water for the plain `600 g` mix. A straight
scale-up to `750 g` total dry base points to about `450 g` water, which is a
good starting point. The extra wholemeal rye and existing grain inclusions may
want a little more, but not so much that the dough loses shape.

House guidance:

- start with `450 g` water,
- mix fully,
- rest `15` minutes,
- then add more water only in small increments if the dough still feels too
  tight.

Target texture:

- soft and tacky,
- slightly grainy from the inclusions,
- elastic enough to shape,
- sticky enough to need a scraper,
- not slack enough to flatten into a puddle.

## Method

1. Mix the dry ingredients. Combine the `600 g` premix, the `150 g` wholemeal
   rye flour, and the yeast in a large bowl.
2. Add `450 g` lukewarm water. As with the packet guidance, water around `30 C`
   is a good target.
3. Mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest `15` minutes so the rye,
   bran, linseed, and other grain pieces can hydrate.
4. Reassess hydration. If the dough still feels noticeably too stiff, add more
   water `10 g` at a time until it becomes soft and tacky but still
   shape-holding.
5. Knead vigorously for about `8 to 10` minutes, aiming for a cohesive, smooth
   dough with visible elasticity. Because of the rye and inclusions, it will
   not feel as sleek as a plain white dough.
6. Return the dough to a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let it rise until
   roughly doubled and lively. The packet suggests about `40` minutes; in
   practice `40 to 60` minutes is a more useful house range.
7. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured bench and degas it gently.
8. Pre-shape into a round and rest `10` minutes.
9. Final-shape into a taut boule and place seam-side up in a well-floured
   banneton.
10. Final-proof until clearly fuller and a floured finger poke springs back
    slowly. Do not insist on an exaggerated full double if the dough already
    feels well aerated.
11. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at `240 C` fan-forced for `30 to 45`
    minutes.
12. Turn the loaf out, score as desired, and load into the Dutch oven.
13. Bake covered for `20` minutes at `230 C` fan-forced.
14. Remove the lid, reduce to `210 C` fan-forced, and bake for a further `22 to
    27` minutes until well browned and about `96 C` internal.
15. Cool fully before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: `750 g`
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: `240 C` fan-forced for `30 to 45` minutes
- Bake temperatures: `230 C` covered, then `210 C` uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: `20` minutes covered, `22 to 27` minutes
  uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: even, grain-speckled, and sliceable
- Crust: dark brown and moderately crisp
- Flavor: wheaty, grainy, mildly malty, with a clearer rye note than the stock
  mix alone
- Shape: rounded boule with enough strength to hold a respectable profile

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough is happiest closer to `450 g` or `470 g` water
- Whether the added rye shortens the optimal final proof
- Whether a slightly shorter uncovered bake leaves the crumb too damp
- Whether the loaf would benefit from a lighter degas before final shaping

## Notes

- The added wholemeal rye is a house customization, not part of the
  manufacturer's formula.
- Wholemeal rye was chosen over wholemeal spelt because it fits the premix's
  own grain profile better and reinforces the dark, German-style grain-bread
  character.
- Because the mix already includes salt and improvers, avoid extra additions
  until the base house version has been tested.
