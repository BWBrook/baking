# Dark Rye Commercial Blend Dutch-Oven Boule

## Goal

Turn the commercial dark rye bread mix into a single well-shaped banneton
boule, baked in the Dutch oven rather than as two smaller tin loaves.

## Source basis

This recipe is based on the manufacturer's 500 g-mix formula and method, then
adapted to fit the current house workflow:

- one loaf rather than two tin loaves,
- banneton proof instead of tin proof,
- Dutch-oven baking in a fan-forced electric oven,
- tighter control of water to avoid overshooting hydration.

## Mix notes

The blend already includes:

- wheat flour,
- dark rye flour,
- dark malt barley flour,
- added wheat gluten,
- salt,
- sugar,
- soy flour,
- oils,
- dough improvers and conditioners.

That means the mix is designed to be more forgiving and more structurally
capable than a simple flour-only rye blend.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dark rye commercial bread mix | 500 g | Half of the 1 kg bag |
| Instant dry yeast | 2 tsp | Manufacturer suggests `SNK Gold Standard Yeast`; any reliable instant yeast is fine |
| Water | 250 to 300 g | Start at 250 g and adjust carefully |
| Oil | 2 tsp | Neutral oil or the recommended sunflower oil |
| Rice flour or flour for dusting | as needed | Best for banneton release |
| Seeds | optional | Only if desired for topping |

## Hydration guidance

The manufacturer's 335 ml water guidance works as an upper ceiling, not as the
best automatic starting point for a banneton boule.

House guidance:

- start with about 250 g to 260 g water,
- mix thoroughly,
- rest 10 to 15 minutes,
- then add water in small increments only if the dough feels too dry.

Target texture:

- tacky,
- cohesive,
- self-supporting,
- sticky enough to need a scraper,
- not slack enough to puddle on the bench.

## Method

1. Mix the dry ingredients. Combine the bread mix and yeast in a large bowl.
2. Add 250 g water and the oil. Mix until no dry mix remains.
3. Rest the dough 10 to 15 minutes so the rye, soy, and improvers can hydrate.
4. Reassess hydration. If the dough feels too stiff to hold together cleanly,
   add more water 10 g to 15 g at a time until it becomes tacky but
   shape-holding.
5. Do the first stretch and fold on a lightly dampened or lightly oiled bench.
   Use wet hands and a scraper rather than extra flour.
6. Return to a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and rest 20 minutes.
7. Repeat the stretch and fold 1 or 2 more times at 20-minute intervals.
8. Bulk ferment until the dough is clearly puffy and aerated, roughly a 70% to
   90% rise. Do not wait for an exaggerated full double if the dough already
   looks lively.
9. Turn out onto a lightly floured bench and pre-shape into a round. Rest 10
   minutes.
10. Final-shape into a taut boule with decent surface tension.
11. Place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton. Rice flour mixed with a
    little wheat or rye flour works best for release.
12. Final-proof until a floured finger poke springs back slowly without filling
    in immediately. If it stays sunken, it has gone too far.
13. Preheat the Dutch oven and lid at 230 C fan-forced for 30 to 45 minutes.
14. Turn the loaf out, score as desired, and load into the Dutch oven. If using
    seeds, apply them before loading with only a very light mist if needed.
15. Bake covered for 20 minutes at 230 C fan-forced.
16. Remove the lid, reduce to 205 C fan-forced, and bake for a further 20 to
    25 minutes until deep brown and about 96 to 98 C internal.
17. Cool fully before slicing. One to two hours is the minimum; longer is often
    better for this kind of loaf.

## Baking setup

- Total mix: 500 g premix
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: preheated Dutch oven
- Preheat: 230 C fan-forced for 30 to 45 minutes
- Bake temperatures: 230 C covered, then 205 C uncovered
- Covered or uncovered phases: 20 minutes covered, 20 to 25 minutes uncovered

## Result targets

- Crumb: even and sliceable, not gummy
- Crust: deep brown and well coloured
- Flavor: malty, dark, mildly rye-forward
- Shape: round boule with enough lift to hold a clean profile

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough is happiest closer to 260 g or 290 g water
- Whether the dough over-proofs before a full visual double
- Whether a shorter uncovered bake leaves the crumb too damp
- Whether the mix benefits from a slightly longer cooling period before slicing

## Notes

- Because the mix already contains salt, sugar, oils, gluten, and conditioners,
  this recipe does not add any of those separately.
- Avoid adding bench flour freely during handling; that can distort the true
  hydration and leave the crumb heavier than necessary.
- This recipe is intentionally optimized for the house Dutch-oven workflow, not
  for the manufacturer's two-loaf tin method.
