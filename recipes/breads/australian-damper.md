# Australian Damper

## Goal

A house-style oven damper that keeps close to the traditional Australian bush
bread spirit while still baking well in the home kitchen. This version is the
modern oven adaptation: chemically leavened, mixed lightly, and baked as a
round loaf in the cast-iron skillet. A stricter campfire-coals variant is
included at the end.

## Historical basis

- Colonial and bush damper was traditionally a simple bread made from flour,
  water, and salt, cooked in hot ashes, coals, or a camp oven.
- Modern Australian damper recipes often use self-raising flour or other
  raising agents, and may include butter and milk.
- Indigenous Australian baking traditions long predate colonial damper and
  should not be collapsed into the same origin story, even though both involve
  breads cooked with coals, stone, or ashes.

See `research/2026-04-06-australian-damper.md` for the source note behind this
recipe.

## Modern house choices

- Uses plain flour plus baking powder instead of self-raising flour, because
  that fits the current pantry more directly.
- Uses a little butter for tenderness; this suits the oven version well.
- Uses the house cast-iron skillet as the main baking vessel.
- Keeps the dough fairly simple and only lightly worked, so it stays recognizably
  in the damper family rather than turning into a kneaded loaf bread.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Grams | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain wheat flour | 500 | Main flour |
| Baking powder | 18 | Equivalent to using self-raising flour as the lifting base |
| Fine salt | 8 |  |
| Cold butter | 50 | For the oven version; cut into small cubes |
| Milk | 140 |  |
| Water | 150 to 170 | Start with 150 g |
| Extra flour | as needed | For light dusting only |

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 210 C fan-forced.
2. Lightly grease the cast-iron skillet, or line it with baking paper.
3. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, and salt.
4. Rub in the butter until the mixture looks like rough crumbs. Do not work it
   as finely as pastry.
5. Add the milk and 150 g water. Mix lightly with a spoon or dough scraper
   until a rough dough forms.
6. If dry flour remains, add a little more water at a time until the dough just
   comes together. It should be soft but not wet.
7. Turn onto a lightly floured bench and knead only briefly, just enough to
   bring it into a cohesive round. Overworking makes damper tougher.
8. Shape into a round about 4 to 5 cm thick and place it in the skillet.
9. Cut a deep cross in the top, or make 4 to 6 deep slashes. This helps the
   heat reach the centre and gives the classic damper look.
10. Bake for 15 minutes at 210 C fan-forced.
11. Reduce to 190 C fan-forced and bake for a further 18 to 25 minutes, until
    the loaf is golden, sounds hollow when tapped, and feels set through the
    middle.
12. Cool at least 20 to 30 minutes before cutting, though it is best eaten
    still slightly warm.

## Baking setup

- Total flour target: 500 g
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Default shelf: middle shelf
- Vessel: cast-iron skillet
- Bake temperatures: 210 C, then 190 C

## Result targets

- Crumb: close, tender, and slightly crumbly rather than airy
- Crust: golden and rustic
- Flavor: simple wheaty bread with a slight richness from the butter
- Shape: round, thick loaf with a deep cross

## Campfire variant

For a stricter older-style bush version:

- omit the baking powder, milk, and butter,
- use 500 g plain flour, 8 g salt, and about 280 g to 300 g water,
- mix to a firm dough,
- shape into a flat round,
- cook in a camp oven among hot coals or in hot ashes until baked through.

This version will be denser and plainer, but it is closer to the classic flour,
water, and salt tradition described in older Australian references.

## Notes

- If you want a closer self-raising-flour style, replace the plain flour plus
  baking powder with 500 g self-raising flour and keep the salt.
- Butter suits the oven version, but it is not essential to the older bush
  idea.
- Golden syrup, butter, jam, or savory camp-style accompaniments all make sense
  with damper.
