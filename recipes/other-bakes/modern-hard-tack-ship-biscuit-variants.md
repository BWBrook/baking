# Modern Hard Tack and Ship's Biscuit Variants

## Goal

Make historically bent, long-lasting savoury biscuits inspired by hardtack and
ship's biscuit, but adapted for a modern home kitchen: thinner, more palatable,
more varied in flour choice, and not baked for an absurd length of time.

## Concept note

Traditional hardtack and ship's biscuit were fundamentally flour, water, and
salt, baked dry for transport and storage. This recipe keeps that dry-biscuit
logic but treats it as a modern pantry snack and soup/stew companion rather
than a strict naval reconstruction.

These are not meant to last for years. The house target is:

- dry and crisp,
- sturdy enough to keep well,
- simple enough to repeat,
- and flavourful enough to eat voluntarily.

See `research/2026-06-09-modern-hard-tack-ship-biscuit-variants.md` for the
source note behind this recipe.

## Master method

Use any one of the flour variants below.

1. Preheat the oven to `180 C` fan-forced. Line a baking tray with baking
   paper.
2. Mix the dry ingredients thoroughly.
3. Add the oil or honey if using, then add the lower amount of water.
4. Mix and knead briefly until the dough comes together. It should be firm,
   not soft. Add more water only `5 g` to `10 g` at a time.
5. Rest covered for `10` minutes.
6. Roll to about `4` to `5 mm` thick. Thicker biscuits keep the historic
   feeling but are harder to dry properly.
7. Cut into squares or rounds about `5` to `7 cm` across.
8. Dock deeply with a fork or skewer. Push through, not just across the
   surface.
9. Bake for `18` minutes.
10. Flip the biscuits, reduce the oven to `160 C` fan-forced, and bake for
    another `14` to `18` minutes.
11. For better keeping, reduce to `120 C` fan-forced and dry for another `20`
    to `30` minutes, or switch the oven off and leave the biscuits inside for
    `30` to `45` minutes.
12. Cool completely on a rack before storing.

## Variant 1: Cabin Bread Baseline

This is the plainest and most ship's-biscuit-adjacent version while still being
more edible than strict hardtack.

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 180 g | Main structure |
| Plain wholemeal bread wheat flour | 90 g | Grain flavour |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 30 g | Historical bent and darker note |
| Fine salt | 5 g |  |
| Olive oil | 0 to 10 g | Optional; omit for best keeping |
| Water | 135 to 155 g | Start at `135 g` |

Expected result:

- crisp,
- plain,
- wheat-forward,
- suitable with cheese, soup, stew, or butter.

## Variant 2: Maslin Rye-Spelt Sea Biscuit

This is the most obviously mixed-grain version and fits the repo's usual
preference for maslin-style flour blends.

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 120 g | Structural support |
| Plain wholemeal bread wheat flour | 60 g | Wheat flavour |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 75 g | Main darker grain |
| Wholemeal spelt flour | 45 g | Nutty, softer grain note |
| Fine salt | 5 g |  |
| Caraway, fennel, or dried rosemary | 1 to 2 tsp | Optional but recommended |
| Olive oil | 10 g | Optional but useful for palatability |
| Water | 145 to 170 g | Start at `145 g` |

Expected result:

- darker,
- more aromatic,
- less brittle than the plain version,
- good with cheese, smoked fish, pickles, or soup.

## Variant 3: Blue Masa and Quinoa Expedition Biscuit

This is the least historical in a naval sense, but it keeps the journey-food
idea while using the pantry's American-grain direction.

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard white flour | 150 g | Structural support |
| Blue masa harina | 75 g | Nixtamalized maize flavour |
| Quinoa flour | 45 g | Nutty, earthy note |
| Medium-grind cornmeal | 30 g | Texture |
| Fine salt | 5 g |  |
| Ground pepper or dried chilli | to taste | Optional |
| Olive oil | 10 g | Optional but recommended |
| Water | 150 to 175 g | Start at `150 g` |

Expected result:

- crisp and grainy,
- lightly maize-forward,
- more crumbly than the wheat and rye versions,
- good with beans, chilli, stew, or strong cheese.

First bake note:

- A reported 2026-06 bake of this variant was superb and genuinely hard after
  residual-heat oven drying. The baker estimated final moisture was probably no
  more than about `10%`, though this was not measured.

## Variant 4: Barley-Oat Pantry Biscuit

This is the gentlest, slightly sweeter grain version. It will be more fragile
than the wheat baseline but should be pleasant as a simple savoury biscuit.

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Barley flour | 75 g | Mild sweetness |

| Plain wholemeal bread wheat flour | 30 g | Grain support |

Expected result:

- mild,

- less tooth-threatening,
- useful as a snack biscuit or with soup.

## Storage guidance

- Cool completely before storing.
- Store only when the biscuits feel dry all the way through.
- Use an airtight jar or tin in a cool, dry cupboard.
- For the best keeping batch, omit oil and honey.
- For the best eating batch, use a modest `10 g` olive oil per `300 g` flour.
- If any biscuit softens in storage, re-crisp in a low oven before eating.
- Discard if there is any visible mould, dampness, musty smell, or rancid oil
  smell.

## Baking setup

- Tin, tray, skillet, or other vessel: baking tray
- Oven setting: fan-forced bake
- Temperature: `180 C`, then `160 C`, optional final dry at `120 C`
- Time: about `32` to `36` minutes active baking, plus optional `20` to `45`
  minutes drying

## Result targets

- Texture: dry, crisp, and sturdy, but not jaw-breaking
- Flavor: simple grain flavour with optional herbs or spices
- Structure: firm enough to store and travel without crumbling immediately
- Appearance: pale gold to brown, depending on flour blend

## Variables to watch

- Whether `4 mm` or `5 mm` is the best thickness
- Whether oil is worth the reduced storage potential
- Which flour blend stays crisp longest
- Whether the blue masa and quinoa version needs a small amount of psyllium if
  it proves too crumbly
- Whether residual-heat oven drying should be shortened when the goal is a
  more palatable crisp biscuit rather than true hard tack
- Whether a lighter "puffed tack" version works best with `10 g` to `12 g`
  baking powder per `300 g` flour, with `15 g` as the upper experimental end

## Notes

- Keep fresh herbs, cheese, eggs, yogurt, butter, and milk out of these if the
  goal is long keeping.
- Honey or molasses can be trialled later for flavour, but the first round
  should stay mostly dry and savoury.
- If making these mainly for immediate eating, the oil version will probably be
  nicer. If making them for pantry keeping, the fat-free version is the better
  first test.
