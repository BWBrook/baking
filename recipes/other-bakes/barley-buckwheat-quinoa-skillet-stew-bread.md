# Barley, Buckwheat, and Quinoa Skillet Stew Bread

## Goal

Make a savory skillet quick bread built around barley flour, buckwheat flour,
and quinoa flour, aimed squarely at soups and stews rather than sweet baking.

## Source basis

This house recipe is informed by:

- the current pantry and skillet workflow,
- Bob's Red Mill guidance on alternative-flour substitution percentages,
- King Arthur's testing notes that quick breads are among the easiest formats
  for higher ancient-grain substitutions,
- and the practical need to keep quinoa from taking over the flavor.

See `research/2026-04-07-barley-buckwheat-quinoa-breads.md` for the supporting
note.

## Blend logic

The house default blend is:

- `60%` barley flour,
- `20%` buckwheat flour,
- `20%` quinoa flour.

Reason:

- barley gives sweetness and a softer crumb,
- buckwheat contributes earthy depth without becoming too chalky,
- quinoa stays below the point where it is likely to feel too dry or dominant.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barley flour | 324 g | Main flour |
| Buckwheat flour | 108 g | Flavor and structure |
| Quinoa flour | 108 g | Nutty accent |
| Psyllium husk | 10 g | Strongly recommended for cohesion |
| Baking powder | 18 g | Main leavener |
| Baking soda | 4 g | Supports lift with the yogurt |
| Fine salt | 10 g |  |
| Honey | 15 g | Optional but recommended for balance and browning |
| Greek yogurt | 300 g |  |
| Water | 250 to 270 g | Start with `250 g`, then adjust after resting |
| Eggs | 3 large | About `150 g` out of shell |
| Olive oil | 55 g | Default fat |
| Dried thyme or rosemary | 1 tsp | Optional but well suited to stews |
| Extra oil for skillet | as needed | For greasing |

## Method

1. Preheat the oven to `195 C` fan-forced. Place the `10"` cast-iron skillet in
   the oven while it heats if you want a firmer edge crust.
2. Lightly oil the hot or room-temperature skillet.
3. In a large bowl whisk together the barley flour, buckwheat flour, quinoa
   flour, psyllium husk, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and herbs if using.
4. In a second bowl whisk together the honey, Greek yogurt, `250 g` water,
   eggs, and olive oil.
5. Pour the wet mixture into the dry ingredients and stir thoroughly until no
   dry pockets remain. The batter should be thick and cohesive, more like a
   heavy muffin batter than a pourable cake batter.
6. Rest the batter `10` minutes so the flours and psyllium can hydrate.
7. Reassess consistency. If the batter has become too stiff to spread
   comfortably, add some or all of the remaining `20 g` water.
8. Transfer the batter to the skillet and smooth the top. Score or mark a
   shallow cross if you want a cleaner split.
9. Bake on the middle shelf for `35 to 42` minutes, until well browned and a
   skewer comes out clean or with only a few moist crumbs.
10. Cool in the skillet for at least `15` minutes before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Tin, tray, skillet, or other vessel: `10"` cast-iron skillet with lid kept
  off
- Oven setting: fan-forced bake
- Temperature: `195 C`
- Time: `35 to 42` minutes

## Result targets

- Texture: moist, tender, and hearty rather than fluffy
- Flavor: earthy, nutty, and savory enough to suit soups and stews
- Structure: sliceable and cohesive, not sandy or crumbly
- Appearance: bronzed top with a darker grain-speckled interior

## Variables to watch

- Whether the batter wants the full extra `20 g` water after resting
- Whether the quinoa note feels balanced at `20%`
- Whether the bread is better with or without the herbs
- Whether `10 g` psyllium is enough for your preferred sliceability

## Notes

- Psyllium is part of the default formula here because it materially improves
  cohesion in a flour blend with very little useful gluten.
- If you want a stronger buckwheat note later, reduce barley and increase
  buckwheat modestly rather than raising quinoa first.
- This bread should be at its best warm, or reheated lightly before serving
  with stew.
