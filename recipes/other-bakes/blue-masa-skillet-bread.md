# Blue Masa Skillet Bread

## Goal

Make a nixtamalized-corn skillet quick bread using blue masa harina as the main
corn ingredient, so it stands as a clear contrast to the standard medium-grind
cornmeal cornbread in the repo.

## Tradition note

This is best understood as a modern hybrid rather than a single strict
traditional North American bread type.

It draws on:

- the deep Mesoamerican tradition of nixtamalized masa,
- baked masa forms such as `gorditas de horno`,
- and the modern practice of using masa harina in cornbread-style baked goods.

So it is not baseless invention, but it is also not being claimed here as a
direct historical replica of one canonical old dish.

See `research/2026-04-07-blue-masa-skillet-bread.md` for the supporting note.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Uses blue masa harina as the dominant corn ingredient for clear nixtamalized
  flavor and color.
- Uses a smaller amount of plain white flour for structure, because a fully
  masa-based skillet quick bread would move closer to cake or dense masa pie
  territory.
- Uses Greek yogurt plus water as the acidic dairy component, since that fits
  the current pantry better than buttermilk.
- Uses olive oil as the default house fat.
- Includes a short batter rest, because masa harina hydrates quickly and
  benefits from a little standing time.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue masa harina | 250 g | Main corn ingredient |
| Plain white flour | 170 g | Structural support |
| Sugar | 25 g | Enough to round the flavour without making it sweet cake |
| Baking powder | 12 g | Main leavener |
| Baking soda | 4 g | Supports lift with the yogurt |
| Fine salt | 8 g |  |
| Greek yogurt | 250 g |  |
| Water | 220 g | Add a little more only if needed |
| Eggs | 3 large | About `150 g` out of shell |
| Olive oil | 80 g |  |
| Extra oil for skillet | as needed | For greasing |

## Method

1. Preheat the oven to `200 C` fan-forced. Place the `10"` cast-iron skillet in
   the oven while it heats if you want a stronger bottom crust.
2. Lightly oil the hot or room-temperature skillet.
3. In a large bowl whisk together the blue masa harina, white flour, sugar,
   baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
4. In a second bowl whisk together the Greek yogurt, water, eggs, and olive oil
   until smooth.
5. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and mix until no dry
   pockets remain. The batter should be thick, smooth, and spoonable.
6. Rest the batter `10` minutes so the masa can fully hydrate.
7. Reassess consistency. If the batter has become too stiff to spread
   comfortably, add `10 g` to `20 g` extra water.
8. Transfer the batter to the skillet and smooth the top.
9. Bake on the middle shelf for `25 to 32` minutes, until the top is set and a
   skewer inserted in the centre comes out mostly clean.
10. Rest in the skillet for at least `10` minutes before slicing.

## Baking setup

- Tin, tray, skillet, or other vessel: `10"` cast-iron skillet with lid kept
  off
- Oven setting: fan-forced bake
- Temperature: `200 C`
- Time: `25 to 32` minutes

## Result targets

- Texture: tender but slightly denser than standard cornbread
- Flavor: distinctly nixtamalized, earthy, and a little grassy-sweet from the
  blue masa
- Structure: slices cleanly, but with more cohesive chew than ordinary
  cornmeal cornbread
- Appearance: muted blue-violet to grey-blue crumb with a bronzed top

## Variables to watch

- Whether the batter wants the extra `10 g` to `20 g` water after resting
- Whether `170 g` white flour is the right support level
- Whether the `200 C` fan bake gives enough colour without drying the crumb
- Whether you prefer a slightly lower sugar level for a more savoury result

## Notes

- This should taste clearly different from the standard cornmeal skillet bread.
- Blue masa behaves differently from medium-grind cornmeal: it hydrates more
  completely, gives a smoother batter, and brings a stronger nixtamalized
  flavor.
- The plain white flour is there for structure, not to dominate the loaf.
