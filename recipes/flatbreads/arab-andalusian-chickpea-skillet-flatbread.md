# Arab-Andalusian Chickpea Skillet Flatbread

## Goal

Make a true unleavened chickpea flatbread that feels at home in the broad
Middle Eastern and Mediterranean world: olive-oil driven, deeply savory, and
easy enough for a first flatbread attempt.

## Tradition note

This recipe is best understood as a modern house adaptation built from two
related historical threads:

- the Arab-Andalusian chickpea pancakes recorded in medieval Muslim Spain,
- and the later socca or farinata family of chickpea-and-olive-oil skillet
  breads around the western Mediterranean.

So it is not being claimed here as one strict canonical Levantine or Arabic
flatbread. It is an intentionally unleavened chickpea bread that keeps that
broader regional spirit.

See `research/2026-04-07-unleavened-flatbreads-chickpea-quinoa-buckwheat.md`
for the source note behind this recipe.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Keeps the bread truly unleavened, even though one medieval chickpea-pancake
  source uses yeast.
- Uses the cast-iron skillet in the oven so no flipping is required.
- Keeps the flavor base simple: chickpea flour, olive oil, salt, and optional
  warm spices if they are already in the kitchen.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chickpea flour | 180 g | Main flour |
| Water | 360 g | Room temperature |
| Olive oil | 15 g | For the batter |
| Fine salt | 6 g |  |
| Ground cumin | 1 tsp | Optional but very well suited if available |
| Crushed fennel seed | 1/2 tsp | Optional; nods to the Arab-Andalusian source |
| Extra olive oil | 10 g | For the hot skillet |

## Method

1. Heat the oven to `230 C` fan-forced with the `10"` cast-iron skillet inside.
2. In a bowl, whisk together the chickpea flour, salt, and optional spices.
3. Whisk in the water and `15 g` olive oil until the batter is smooth.
4. Rest the batter for `30` minutes so the chickpea flour fully hydrates.
5. Carefully remove the hot skillet, add the remaining `10 g` olive oil, and
   swirl to coat.
6. Pour in the batter and tilt the skillet so it settles into an even round.
7. Bake on the middle shelf for `12 to 16` minutes, until the flatbread is set
   through the middle and the edges are clearly browning.
8. If you want a more street-bread style finish, give it `1 to 2` minutes
   under a hot grill at the end.
9. Rest `5` minutes, then loosen and slide onto a board for slicing.

## Cooking setup

- Vessel: preheated `10"` cast-iron skillet
- Oven setting: fan-forced bake, with optional short grill finish
- Temperature: `230 C` fan-forced
- Time: `12 to 16` minutes, plus optional `1 to 2` minutes under grill

## Result targets

- Texture: tender in the centre with crisping edges
- Flavor: olive-oily, chickpea-rich, and savory
- Structure: sliceable wedges rather than a fluffy bread crumb
- Appearance: bronzed top with slightly darker edge blistering

## Variables to watch

- Whether your oven prefers the grill finish for stronger top colour
- Whether you want the optional cumin and fennel version or the plainer one
- Whether the bread is at its best cut into wedges or torn into strips

## Notes

- This is probably the easiest of the three new flatbreads for a first attempt.
- If the batter seems thick after resting, whisk in `10 g` to `20 g` more
  water before it goes into the skillet.
- Best eaten warm, with olive oil, yogurt, soft cheese, or soup.
