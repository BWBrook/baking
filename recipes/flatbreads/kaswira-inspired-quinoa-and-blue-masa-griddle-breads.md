# Kaswira-Inspired Quinoa and Blue Masa Griddle Breads

## Goal

Make a truly unleavened quinoa flatbread that feels Andean in spirit, using
quinoa as the main flour and a smaller amount of blue masa harina to help it
handle more confidently on the griddle.

## Tradition note

This recipe is a modern adaptation inspired by documented Uru-Aymara
`qarasiña` or `kaswira`, a quinoa-flour food from the Lake Titicaca region.
That traditional form uses quinoa flour, salt, water, oil or fat, and limewater,
and is flattened thin and fried.

The house version is not a strict reconstruction. Instead, it keeps the quinoa
centre of gravity, drops the limewater because modern quinoa flour is already
debittered, and uses a modest amount of blue masa harina as the handling aid
instead of wheat flour.

See `research/2026-04-07-unleavened-flatbreads-chickpea-quinoa-buckwheat.md`
for the source note behind this recipe.

## Modern adaptation choices

- Keeps the bread truly unleavened.
- Uses blue masa harina as the support flour so the recipe stays closer to a
  pre-wheat American grain logic.
- Makes small individual griddle breads rather than one large fragile round.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quinoa flour | 210 g | Main flour |
| Blue masa harina | 90 g | Support flour and flavour accent |
| Fine salt | 7 g |  |
| Olive oil | 18 g | Mixed into the dough |
| Hot water | 280 to 300 g | Start with `280 g` |
| Extra oil | as needed | For the skillet and hands |

## Method

1. In a bowl, mix the quinoa flour, blue masa harina, and salt.
2. Add the olive oil and `280 g` hot water. Stir until a shaggy dough forms.
3. Rest `10` minutes so the flours hydrate fully.
4. Knead briefly in the bowl or on a lightly oiled surface until the dough is
   cohesive. If it cracks badly, work in a little more hot water.
5. Divide into `6` equal pieces.
6. Press each piece between two sheets of baking paper or with lightly oiled
   hands into a round about `12 to 14 cm` across. Keep them on the thicker side
   at first; very thin rounds are harder to move.
7. Heat the cast-iron skillet over medium heat and oil it very lightly.
8. Cook the flatbreads one at a time for about `2 to 3` minutes on the first
   side and `1 1/2 to 2 1/2` minutes on the second, until speckled and set.
9. Stack under a cloth while you finish the batch so they stay softer.

## Cooking setup

- Vessel: lightly oiled cast-iron skillet
- Heat source: stovetop
- Heat level: medium
- Time: about `4 to 5` minutes per flatbread

## Result targets

- Texture: rustic and tender when warm, firmer after cooling
- Flavor: earthy quinoa first, then a quieter nixtamalized maize note
- Structure: cohesive enough to fold or tear, but not as elastic as wheat
- Appearance: pale tan to grey-gold with darker toasted spots

## Variables to watch

- Whether the dough wants the full `300 g` water
- Whether the `70/30` quinoa-to-masa balance feels right after the first trial
- Whether slightly smaller rounds are easier for your first batch

## Notes

- This is the trickiest of the three new flatbreads, but still very doable.
- If the first round cracks when lifted, make the next ones slightly smaller
  and a touch thicker.
- These are best eaten warm, when they are most pliable.
