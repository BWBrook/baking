# Kaswira-Inspired Quinoa and Blue Masa Oven Skillet Round

## Goal

Make a single oven-baked skillet round inspired by the kaswira-style quinoa and
blue masa griddle breads, but with a firmer, savoury, biscuit-like texture that
can be cut into wedges.

## Tradition note

This is a modern house offshoot of the kaswira-inspired griddle breads. It is
not a strict historical or ethnographic reconstruction. The key ingredients
remain quinoa flour, blue masa harina, water, salt, and oil, but the cooking
method is now an oven-baked cast-iron round rather than small griddle cakes.

See `recipes/flatbreads/kaswira-inspired-quinoa-and-blue-masa-griddle-breads.md`
for the original small-griddle-bread version.

## Development note

This version comes from two kitchen trials:

- 2026-05-09: first oven round was delicious but pasty in the centre.
- 2026-05-10: lower water, a `2:1` quinoa-to-blue-masa ratio, herbs, pepper,
  and harder baking produced a well-set savoury biscuit-like bread.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quinoa flour | 165 g | Main flour |
| Blue masa harina | 85 g | Support flour and flavour accent |
| Fine salt | 5 g |  |
| Mixed dried herbs | 1 to 2 tsp | Optional but recommended for the savoury version |
| Ground black pepper | to taste | Optional |
| Olive oil | 15 g | Mixed into the dough |
| Hot water | 215 to 230 g | Start with `215 g`; add only if needed |
| Extra olive oil | as needed | For oiling the skillet and surface |

## Method

1. Lightly oil the cold cast-iron skillet.
2. In a bowl, mix the quinoa flour, blue masa harina, salt, herbs, and pepper.
3. Add the olive oil and `215 g` hot water. Stir until the dough comes
   together.
4. Rest `10` to `15` minutes so the flours hydrate.
5. Assess the dough. It should be pressable and slightly crumbly, not soft and
   pasty. Add more hot water only if it cracks so much that it will not hold
   together.
6. Press the dough into the skillet as a round about `8` to `9` inches across.
   It does not need to fill the whole skillet.
7. Lightly oil the top and dock it a few times with a fork.
8. Cover with the skillet lid and place in a preheated `205 C` to `210 C`
   fan-forced oven.
9. Bake covered for `10` to `12` minutes.
10. Remove the lid and bake uncovered for another `12` to `18` minutes, until
    the round feels set through.
11. If the top is still pale, finish under the grill for `2` to `5` minutes,
    watching closely.
12. Rest `10` minutes before cutting into wedges.

## Cooking setup

- Vessel: `10"` cast-iron skillet with lid
- Skillet start: cold or only gently warm, lightly oiled
- Oven style: electric fan-forced oven
- Temperature: `205 C` to `210 C` fan-forced
- Covered phase: `10` to `12` minutes
- Uncovered phase: `12` to `18` minutes
- Optional finish: `2` to `5` minutes under the grill

## Result targets

- Texture: fully set, crumbly, and savoury-biscuit-like rather than soft and
  bendable
- Flavor: earthy quinoa, blue masa sweetness, herbs, and pepper
- Structure: sliceable into wedges without a pasty centre
- Appearance: browned top and bottom, with a firm centre

## Variables to watch

- Whether the water should sit closer to `215 g` or `230 g`
- Whether herbs and pepper should become standard or remain optional
- Whether the lid-on phase should be shortened if the centre stays too moist
- Whether a thinner `8"` round or a wider `9"` round gives the best set

## Notes

- This is not trying to replace the original griddle-bread version. It is a
  different house variant.
- The first successful version ate more like a traditional savoury biscuit than
  a soft flatbread, and that is now treated as the intended direction.
- Keep the skillet cold or only gently warm for loading. The dough is easier to
  press cleanly before the iron is hot.
