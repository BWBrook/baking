# Baseline Practice

This note records the current default assumptions for recipe design in this
repository.

## Current defaults

- Yeasted doughs default to instant dry yeast.
- Chemical leavening defaults to baking powder.
- Olive oil is the preferred fat unless butter clearly gives the better result.
- Rice flour is mainly a bench and banneton dusting flour.
- Main baking environment is a fan-forced home electric oven.
- Strong white bread flour is often used as the structural support in mixed
  loaves rather than as the sole flour.
- Standard yeasted bread format is usually a Dutch-oven loaf built around 750 g
  total flour.
- Standard quick-bread format is usually a cast-iron-skillet bake built around
  500 g to 650 g total flour.

## Equipment-centered assumptions

- Covered baking in the Dutch oven is a core method for hearth-style loaves.
- The cast-iron skillet with lid is available as an alternative baking vessel.
- Banneton use is part of the normal workflow for proofed loaves.
- The Dutch oven is the default vessel for yeasted breads unless a recipe has a
  clear reason to do something else.
- The cast-iron skillet is the preferred vessel for quick breads unless a
  different tin or tray is part of the recipe style.

## Open development areas

- Sourdough starter and levain.
- Baking soda plus cream of tartar systems.
- Unleavened and lower-gluten flatbreads.
- Flour-specific handling notes for rye, spelt, barley, quinoa, and chickpea.
- Historical loaf reconstruction from medieval through Georgian periods.
- Ancient-grain experiments using emmer, einkorn, khorasan, and related grains
  if acquired.
