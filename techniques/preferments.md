# Preferments

This note records the main preferment types that are likely to matter in this
repository.

## What a preferment is

A preferment is a portion of flour and liquid, with yeast or natural
microorganisms, that is fermented before the final dough is mixed.

Main benefits:

- more flavour,
- better hydration,
- improved dough handling,
- better keeping quality,
- more flexible fermentation planning.

In this repo, preferments matter both for practical baking and for historical
bread work.

## Quick orientation

The broad family includes:

- sponge,
- poolish,
- biga,
- pâte fermentée,
- levain or sourdough.

The forgotten French one you were thinking of is `pâte fermentée`, meaning
`fermented dough` or `old dough`.

Important nuance:

- `Poolish` is also often called a French preferment.
- `Pâte fermentée` is the old-dough method where a piece of mixed dough is held
  back for the next batch.

They are not the same thing.

## Sponge

`Sponge` is the broadest and oldest-feeling of these terms. In industrial and
professional usage it often means a plastic preferment used in a sponge-and-
dough system. In home baking, the word is used more loosely for many kinds of
pre-fermented flour-water-yeast mixes.

Typical character:

- medium or variable hydration,
- usually yeasted,
- can be short or overnight,
- often used as a practical flavour and fermentation boost.

For this repo:

- the Georgian wholemeal loaf sponge is in this family,
- historical barm-based preparations are also conceptually close to sponge
  methods.

## Poolish

Poolish is a liquid preferment traditionally associated with French bread
making, though its roots are commonly traced through Polish and Viennese baking
into France.

Typical character:

- 100% hydration,
- flour + water + a small amount of yeast,
- no salt,
- usually fermented until bubbly and mature.

What it tends to do:

- improves aroma,
- increases extensibility,
- supports open crumb and thin crust in the right dough,
- suits baguettes, batards, and similar breads.

Poolish is often the best choice when the aim is a lighter, more extensible,
French-leaning loaf.

## Biga

Biga is an Italian preferment, usually stiffer than poolish.

Typical character:

- about 50% to 60% hydration,
- flour + water + a small amount of yeast,
- no salt,
- ripened for several hours or overnight.

What it tends to do:

- strengthens dough,
- supports flavour development,
- gives a drier, nuttier, less slack preferment than poolish,
- suits breads where structure matters.

Biga is often a good fit when a dough needs flavour and maturity without the
same degree of looseness that poolish encourages.

## Pâte fermentée

Pâte fermentée is fermented dough or old dough.

This is the one where a piece of already-mixed dough is held over from one bake
and then added into the next batch.

Because it is literally a piece of finished dough, it contains:

- flour,
- water,
- yeast,
- salt.

That makes it different from poolish and most biga formulas, which usually omit
salt.

Why it matters:

- brings flavour from prior fermentation,
- reinforces continuity between batches,
- is historically and practically close to the old practice of carrying dough
  forward,
- feels sourdough-adjacent in workflow, even though it is not a maintained wild
  culture in the way a true sourdough starter is.

## Levain and sourdough

Levain sits at the edge of this note because you did not specifically ask for
it, but it helps explain the boundary.

- `Pâte fermentée` uses commercial yeast from an already-mixed dough.
- `Levain` or sourdough uses an ongoing culture of wild yeast and bacteria.

So pâte fermentée is old dough, not sourdough, even though both carry ferment
forward from one bake to the next.

## Comparison table

| Type | Hydration | Salt? | Typical personality | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sponge | Variable, often medium | Usually no in simple versions | Broad, flexible, practical | Historical loaves, general flavour lift |
| Poolish | About 100% | No | Loose, aromatic, extensible | Baguettes, French-style breads |
| Biga | About 50% to 60% | No | Stiffer, strengthening, nutty | Italian-style or structure-seeking doughs |
| Pâte fermentée | Final-dough hydration | Yes | Old dough, continuous, balanced | Traditional lean breads, continuity between batches |

## House guidance

For this repo:

- use `sponge` language when the method is historically broad or deliberately
  simple,
- use `poolish` when the goal is a lighter, more extensible wheat dough,
- use `biga` when the dough should gain flavour while staying comparatively
  strong,
- use `pâte fermentée` when a recipe deliberately carries dough forward from
  one bake to the next.

Historical note:

- many older British and colonial breads are more naturally described as
  sponge- or barm-based than as biga or poolish breads.
