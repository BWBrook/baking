# Historical and Mixed-Grain Direction

This note captures a major directional preference in the repository.

## Historical bread interest

Historical baking is a central theme for this project, especially for breads
from:

- medieval traditions,
- early modern baking,
- Georgian-period baking,
- and, in future, older ancient traditions such as Mesopotamian, Egyptian, and
  Roman bread experiments.

These should be treated as a sustained line of exploration, not occasional
novelty bakes.

## Mixed-flour preference

The default bread preference in this repo leans toward mixed-flour loaves
rather than mostly pure white breads.

Typical pattern:

- strong white flour provides structure and gluten support,
- other flours contribute flavor, color, texture, nutrition, or historical
  character,
- even small percentages of secondary flours are worth documenting because they
  may materially change the bread.

Pure white breads still matter, especially when they are the appropriate style,
but they are not the only benchmark for success.

## Historical adaptation rule

When building or refining a historical loaf, try to separate three things:

1. What the source or tradition suggests.
2. What modern home-kitchen constraints require.
3. What actually produced a good result in this kitchen.

That separation will help keep the repo honest and useful.

## Future grain direction

Ancient grains are a likely future expansion area, especially:

- emmer,
- einkorn,
- khorasan,
- and similar grains when they become available.

When these are added, record:

- what the grain was,
- whether it was wholemeal, refined, or blended,
- how it handled during mixing and fermentation,
- whether it improved the loaf in practice.
