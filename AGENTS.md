# AGENTS.md

This repository is a home-baking knowledge base and journal, not a conventional
software codebase. Treat it as a durable notebook for recipes, bake outcomes,
research, and method refinement.

## Purpose

Use this repo to help the user:

- record recipes and recipe revisions,
- log actual bake outcomes,
- maintain pantry and equipment inventories,
- store concise sourced research,
- refine methods over time through evidence.

## Core principles

- Human kitchen experience is the primary evidence.
- Stable recipes and experimental results should not be mixed together.
- Record what happened before proposing what should change.
- Be explicit about uncertainty. Do not overstate claims from memory or theory.
- Keep notes concise, practical, and useful in a home kitchen.
- Treat historical baking as a first-class interest in this repository, not a
  side topic.
- Treat mixed-flour breads as the default area of interest unless the user
  explicitly wants a mostly white loaf.

## Repository expectations

- `inventory/` stores current ingredients, flours, leaveners, and equipment.
- `recipes/` stores working and stable recipes.
- `bake_logs/` stores dated records of individual sessions.
- `research/` stores sourced notes from web research or other references.
- `techniques/` stores general method notes that are not tied to one recipe.

## Content rules

- Prefer metric weights in grams.
- For breads and doughs, use baker's percentages when they help comparison.
- Preserve the difference between a recipe, a bake log, and a research note.
- If the user reports a bake verbally, convert it into a dated log entry first.
- When a recipe is updated after a bake, note what changed and why.
- When web research is done, store the date, question, short synthesis, and
  links to the sources used.
- When working on historical breads, separate original context from modern
  adaptation choices.
- When mixed flours are used, note the structural role of strong white flour
  versus flavor, nutrition, or texture contributions from the other grains.

## Current household defaults

- Default leavening for yeasted doughs: instant dry yeast.
- Default chemical leavening: baking powder.
- Future exploration is welcome for sourdough starter, levain, baking soda plus
  cream of tartar, and unleavened breads.
- Preferred fat is olive oil unless butter is clearly the better choice.
- Rice flour is mainly a handling and dusting flour.
- Strong white flour is commonly used as the structural support flour in mixed
  loaves.
- Historical loaves from medieval through Georgian traditions are a recurring
  priority.
- Ancient-grain experiments are expected in future, including emmer, einkorn,
  and khorasan if acquired.
- Default yeasted bread scale is 750 g total flour, usually baked in the Dutch
  oven.
- Default quick-bread scale is 500 g to 650 g total flour, preferably in the
  cast-iron skillet.

## Inventory guidance

- Keep flour inventory specific and up to date.
- Distinguish pure flours from commercial premixed blends.
- When composition is partly unknown, say so plainly.
- Update inventory files whenever the user mentions new pantry items or tools.

## Editing style

- Keep markdown clean and easy to scan.
- Favor short sections, tables where useful, and direct practical language.
- Do not invent bake outcomes, timings, or recipe performance.
- If a recommendation depends on assumptions, state them.
