# Ingredient Suppliers

This file records known ingredient sources for the baking notebook.

It serves two purposes:

- document the current local baseline for common ingredients,
- keep a practical shopping map for harder-to-find flours and grains.

This file is a working record. Supplier ranges can change, so dated research
notes in `research/` should be used for current online checks before spending.

## Online and specialty sourcing

### Tasmania-first option

- `The Grain Family`
  Strong local option for Tasmanian fresh-milled organic flours and grains,
  especially for khorasan, emmer, and barley variants. Best fit when local
  provenance matters more than broad catalogue convenience.

### Australian online retail options

- `Four Leaf Milling`
  Strong option for Australian-grown and processed organic grains and flours,
  including cracked rye and several less-common grains.
- `Simply No Knead (SNK)`
  Good retail catalogue for specialty flour orders, including ancient grains
  and kibbled rye.
- `Honest to Goodness`
  Good national online option for selected ancient and gluten-free flours such
  as khorasan and sorghum.
- `Goodness Flour`
  Small-batch Victorian mill with interesting wholegrain and specialty flour
  lines, including khorasan, sorghum, and yellow pea flour.
- `Risen Flour`
  Useful for some ancient grains, especially emmer and occasional einkorn.

### Broader supplier and processor options

- `Bio-Oz`
  Important Australian ancient-wheat processor for emmer and khorasan flour.
- `Mirfak`
  Broad Australian processor with a wide range including barley, millet,
  sorghum, and pea flours, but the business appears more wholesale-oriented
  than home-baker-oriented.

## Current sourcing notes

- `Einkorn` looks notably less stable than the other ancient grains and may be
  seasonal or low-volume in Australia.
- `Potato flour` appears harder to source cleanly than potato starch; this
  needs careful label checking because many Australian listings are starch
  rather than whole potato flour.
- `Kibbled rye` appears available from some Australian milling and baking
  suppliers, but stock status may vary.
