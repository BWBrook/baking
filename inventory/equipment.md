# Equipment

This file records the current home-baking equipment baseline.

| Equipment | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Large Le Creuset Dutch oven | Preferred vessel for yeasted hearth-style loaves; round, about 10 inches across, roughly 5 L, and comfortably holds a 750 g flour loaf after oven spring. |
| Heavy cast-iron skillet with lid | Preferred vessel for quick breads; round, about 10 inches across, with a close-fitting lid that seals in steam well, and a current house default of about 500 g to 650 g total flour. |
| Rattan-cane proofing banneton | For final proof support and loaf shaping. |
| Plastic dough scraper | General dough handling and bench work. |
| Razor blade for scoring | For controlled loaf scoring. |
| Whisk | General mixing and batter work. |
| Large mixing bowls | Dough mixing, resting, and fermentation. |
| Built-in electric fan-forced oven | Main baking environment; usually used on the middle shelf with the fan-forced bake setting that runs top and bottom elements plus fan. |

## Current operating notes

- The Dutch oven size is already a good fit for the standard 750 g flour loaf.
- The skillet lid seals closely enough to retain steam well.
- Because both main covered vessels are round, boules and round quick breads
  are currently the natural default shapes.
- The middle shelf is the default rack position.
- For some breads, a brief high grill finish of about 2 to 3 minutes is useful
  for top browning.
- In this oven, fan-forced baking temperatures often work better about 10 C to
  20 C below non-fan recipe guidance.

## Not currently on hand

- No loaf tin yet, so tin loaves are not part of the normal current workflow.
- No pizza stone or baking stone yet.
- A baking tray can still be used for breads and bakes that do not require a
  covered vessel.
